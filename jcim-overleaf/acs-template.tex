\begin{abstract}
Small molecules can adopt different conformations across protein complexes. We benchmark
classical and learned methods that generate conformers without a protein structure to guide
construction of a public conformer dataset. Using CASF and a 22-molecule PLINDER panel,
we assess recovery of observed bound geometries, geometric diversity, energy dispersion,
and structural validity. Four Qwen variants use panel-specific identity and similarity
exclusions during training.

Greater geometric diversity does not consistently correspond to better recovery.
At a target of 1,000 candidates per molecule, Qwen recovers 91.7\% of CASF ref structures
within 0.75 \AA{}, compared with 86.3\% for the next best generator. On PLINDER,
Qwen has the lowest mean reference matching distance, while LoQI has the highest
coverage and sample precision at 0.75 \AA{}. Within Qwen, models using learned coordinate
tokens have lower matching distances than coordinate binning at both model sizes, while
larger models have higher validity.

These findings guide generator selection for conformational resources in structure-based
drug design by relating experimental coverage to diversity and sample concentration
at the intended ensemble size.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Supporting information}

Supporting Information includes core/ref selection counts (Table~\ref{tab:selection-counts}),
PLINDER selection criteria, panel statistics, and molecular identities (Tables~S2--S4),
generator provenance (Table~S5), completed Qwen training exposure
(Table~\ref{tab:qwen-training}), retained ensemble sizes
(Table~\ref{tab:supporting-ensemble-counts}), and energy summaries
(Table~\ref{tab:energy-summary}). Additional methods
document corpus preparation, CoordToken and Qwen training, benchmark exclusions,
generation settings, validity and alignment, raw-geometry energy calculations,
sampling-budget curves, and molecule-level analyses. Supporting figures show the
evaluation panels (Figure~\ref{fig:datasets}), aggregate and molecule-level Qwen comparisons
(Figures~\ref{fig:qwen-size-representation} and \ref{fig:qwen-molecule-effects}), recoveries gained and
lost relative to ChEMBL3D (Figure~\ref{fig:supporting-gains-losses}), joint size and flexibility distributions
(Figure~\ref{fig:supporting-composition}), per-molecule PLINDER coverage, precision, and validity
(Figures~\ref{fig:supporting-plinder-coverage} and \ref{fig:supporting-plinder-validity}), matching
distances across generators (Figure~\ref{fig:plinder-molecule-tracking}), and coverage and precision
across RMSD thresholds (Figure~\ref{fig:plinder-thresholds}).
The companion identity data are provided in \texttt{plinder22-molecules.csv}.
