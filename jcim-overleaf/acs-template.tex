\begin{abstract}
Many small molecules adopt more than one biologically relevant
three-dimensional conformation. The same molecule can have different bound
geometries across proteins or across complexes with the same protein. For
protein-binding applications, a practical goal of conformer generation is
therefore to produce a finite ensemble that represents these observed shapes.
Generating such ensembles without conditioning on a particular protein makes
them reusable across targets and supports the preparation of molecular
libraries and conformational training data. Their usefulness depends on whether
the sampled variation includes geometries relevant to molecular recognition, as
well as on the number and diversity of the structures generated.

We compare classical and learned conformer generators using experimentally
observed bound ligand structures. We first assess recovery of individual
reference conformations for 94 CASF-2016 ligands matched to ChEMBL3D, comparing
generated ensembles with the stored ChEMBL3D conformers at corresponding
candidate counts and at a larger target of 1,000 candidates per molecule. We
examine recovery together with geometric diversity, structural validity,
energetic plausibility, molecular size, and flexibility. We then extend the
evaluation to 23 molecules with multiple bound structures collected from
PLINDER, measuring both coverage of the experimental references and the
fraction of generated conformers near those observations.
