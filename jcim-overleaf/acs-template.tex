\begin{abstract}
Small molecules can adopt different conformations across protein complexes. We benchmark
classical and learned conformer generators to identify procedures for building molecular
libraries that cover these observed bound shapes. Using CASF and a 22-molecule PLINDER
panel, we evaluate experimental recovery alongside geometric diversity, energy dispersion,
and structural validity.

Greater geometric diversity does not consistently improve recovery, and method rankings
depend on sampling budget and matching tolerance. Increasing Qwen's candidate target to
1,000 raises CASF ref recovery within 0.75 \AA{} from 75.6\% to 91.7\%. On PLINDER,
Qwen achieves the lowest mean reference matching distance, while LoQI provides higher
coverage and sample precision.

These results support selecting generators according to ensemble size and the balance
between experimental coverage and sample concentration, providing a basis for constructing
conformational resources for structure-based drug design.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Supporting information}

Supporting Information includes retained ensemble sizes (Table~S1), PLINDER selection
criteria, panel statistics, and molecular identities (Tables~S2--S4), generator
provenance (Table~S5), energy summaries (Table~S6), core/ref selection counts
(Table~S7), and completed Qwen training exposure (Table~S8). Additional methods
document CoordToken training, benchmark exclusions, generation settings, and
validity and alignment procedures. Supporting figures show the
evaluation panels (S1), molecule-level Qwen comparisons (S2), recoveries gained and
lost relative to ChEMBL3D (S3), joint size and flexibility distributions (S4), and
per-molecule PLINDER coverage, precision, and validity (S5--S6).
The companion identity data are provided in \texttt{plinder22-molecules.csv}.
