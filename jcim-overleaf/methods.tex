\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Evaluation design}

Each method generates a conformer ensemble from the ligand identity without receiving
a protein or binding-site structure. PLINDER assesses coverage of multiple bound
structures of the same molecule; CASF core and ref provide comparisons against
individual experimental references and stored ChEMBL3D\cite{nikitin2025loqi} ensembles.
We assess geometric diversity and experimental recovery in all three panels, with
energy and sampling-budget analyses on CASF. Recovery concerns internal ligand
geometry after alignment, not placement within a binding pocket.

\subsection{Experimental references and the ChEMBL3D comparison}

We compare generated and existing conformer ensembles using CASF-2016,\cite{su2019casf} a curated dataset of 285
protein--ligand crystal complexes. The bound ligand geometries provide experimental references for
assessing whether a protein-independent ensemble includes an observed conformation.

We match ligands to ChEMBL3D using canonical heavy-atom isomeric SMILES regenerated
from the supplied MOL2 structures. Matching topology records and stored conformers are
checked against the selected stereoisomer using canonical isomeric SMILES and InChI
stereochemical layers, with stereochemistry assigned from their coordinates. We retain
only matching conformers. Molecules must have a readable reference structure, a usable
matching ChEMBL3D topology, and at least one bond eligible for torsional sampling.
Selection does not use similarity to the experimental coordinates or model performance.

All 285 core ligands were parsed successfully. Exact matching excluded 189 ligands,
the topology requirement excluded one, and torsional eligibility excluded one, leaving
94 core complexes representing 94 ChEMBL parent compounds. The supplied ref archive
contains 4,838 ligand structures. The same procedure excluded 11 parsing/preparation
failures, 3,566 structures without an exact SMILES match, 12 without a usable matching
topology, and 13 without an eligible torsional starting topology, leaving 1,236 complexes.
Supporting Table~\ref{tab:selection-counts} records these sequential exclusions.

For ref, we then retain one crystal per canonical heavy-atom isomeric SMILES identity.
A fixed seeded ranking selects one crystal independently of model outputs and recovery
scores, removing 192 repeated-identity entries. This yields 1,044 crystal complexes
representing 1,042 ChEMBL parent compounds. The collections share 17 parent compounds but
no complex identifiers. Both collections are included in the main tables and the
diversity--proximity, energy, and sampling figures.

For each molecule, the stored ChEMBL3D ensemble establishes how much of the experimental geometry is
already represented in an existing computed dataset. This comparison distinguishes coverage already
available in the reference resource from coverage that can be added by a generation procedure.
Because the comparison is restricted to molecules represented in ChEMBL3D, its conclusions concern
conformational coverage within this intersection rather than coverage of the entire CASF chemical
space.

The multiple-reference evaluation uses a cleaned panel of 22 ligand identities derived from an initial 23-molecule selection from PLINDER
release 2024-06/v2,\cite{durairaj2024plinder} an annotated resource of protein--ligand complexes from the Protein Data Bank.
Ligands were grouped by full InChIKey, screened for physicochemical properties, and ranked
by the number of distinct PDB entries, with system count as the secondary ordering.
The screen retained 25,392 of 51,280 identities. Manual review selected 23 drugs and
medicinal-chemistry ligands from this pool. This is an exploratory selection, not an
official PLINDER split or a random sample. The frozen identity list, screening criteria,
and original frequency counts are archived; Supporting Tables S2--S4 describe the
screen and evaluated panel.

The cleaned collection excludes bortezomib because of covalent annotations and contains 1,067
reference conformations across 22 molecules, with 8--117 per molecule. The supplied structures
were deduplicated at a symmetry-aware RMSD threshold of 0.05 \AA{} and aligned using a fixed
anchor atom triplet for each molecule. Subsequent stereochemical inspection removed two
chloramphenicol records and one staurosporine record with inverted stereocentres. We evaluate
the retained representatives equally within each molecule, without weighting them by the number
of original records in their duplicate clusters. This reduces repeated-coordinate weighting but
does not define biologically distinct conformational states. Supporting Tables S3--S4 describe
the cleaned coordinate collection; original selection frequencies are archived separately.

\subsection{Conformer generators}

The generators represent different approaches to conformational sampling. The main comparison
contains four classical pipelines, five external learned generators, and one Qwen model.
Supporting Table S5 summarizes their representations, training provenance, initialization,
and processing. These methods differ in training data and inference procedures as well as
architecture, so the comparison evaluates the resulting generation pipelines rather than isolating
any single modeling choice.

RDKit ETKDGv3\cite{wang2020etkdg} generates conformers from the molecular graph using distance
geometry and experimental torsional preferences, with stereochemistry enforced. We evaluate
both the embedded coordinates and a pipeline that adds MMFF94s\cite{halgren1999mmff94s}
minimization. The random torsion baseline samples displacements uniformly within
$\pm120^\circ$ around eligible torsions and rejects severe steric clashes.

For CASF, torsion sampling starts from the matched ChEMBL3D topology coordinates, without
using the experimental reference coordinates. Its minimized variant adds MMFF94s
optimization and a further clash check. Raw and minimized CASF pipelines use separately
sampled pools, so they are not paired before-and-after measurements.

The 22-molecule comparison includes RDKit raw and random torsion sampling, each with 1,000
supplied conformers per molecule. RDKit removes the reference coordinates before embedding from the
molecular graph. The torsion baseline builds five ETKDGv3 starting conformations from
isomeric SMILES, without experimental coordinates. From each start, rotatable bonds
are perturbed by up to $\pm120^\circ$, rejecting severe clashes until 200 conformers
are accepted, for 1,000 conformers per molecule. The global random seed is 42, with
reproducible seeds derived for each molecule and starting conformation.
The raw baselines undergo no energy minimization. Their minimized counterparts optimize the
same conformers individually with MMFF94s after rebuilding hydrogens, for up to 500 iterations.
Hydrogens are then removed. All conformers of the 22 included molecules are retained, including
those that reach the iteration limit without convergence; no replacement sampling or additional
clash filtering is applied. For torsion minimization, stereochemistry is checked
and inversions trigger a retry with constraints that preserve chirality.
The four classical pools are evaluated against the same cleaned references. This paired comparison differs from the
separately sampled raw and minimized CASF pools.

The learned methods represent complementary approaches to conformational sampling. Torsional
Diffusion\cite{jing2022torsional} generates conformers by updating torsion angles, while LoQI\cite{nikitin2025loqi} learns low-energy geometries
from ChEMBL3D using stereochemistry-aware coordinate
diffusion. MCF\cite{wang2024mcf} models molecular coordinates as fields over
a graph representation, and NExT-Mol DMT-L\cite{liu2025nextmol} uses a coordinate diffusion transformer; the evaluated
DMT-L adapter does not use the optional MoLlama component. FlowR\cite{cremer2025flowr}
uses flow matching in a ligand-only configuration with the molecular graph held fixed, followed by
force-field cleanup and stereochemistry checks. Their representations,
training resources, and preparation steps are summarized in Supporting Table S5.

Qwen generates a coordinate-enriched molecular sequence from a SMILES prompt using
an autoregressive language model. Our primary generator is initialized from
Qwen3-1.7B-Base\cite{yang2025qwen3} and uses CoordToken,\cite{geikyan2026coordtoken}
a learned tokenizer that represents geometry with one discrete code per atom and one
molecular code. The tokenizer's decoder reconstructs coordinates from these codes
and the molecular graph. We refer to this representation as FSQ, for finite scalar
quantization. The evaluated model is the v2 1.7B FSQ checkpoint at step 41,054.

\subsection{CoordToken and Qwen training}

Training proceeds in two stages. First, CoordToken learns to reconstruct centered
heavy-atom coordinates from a discrete representation conditioned on the molecular
SMILES. We use a separate tokenizer trained for two epochs on 187,398,101 filtered
conformer records, rather than the full-corpus checkpoint reported in the CoordToken
paper. Second, Qwen learns the resulting molecular sequences by next-token prediction,
with the geometry tokenizer and decoder fixed. The primary model completes one epoch
on this export. No subsequent task-specific fine-tuning is applied to the evaluated
v2 checkpoints.

The training collection combines computed conformers and ligand geometries from
protein complexes. Before benchmark-specific filtering, we separate molecular
identities across source training and holdout splits using full Standard InChIKeys,
then remove exact duplicate coordinate-enriched strings within each merged split.
Different conformers and geometric near-duplicates are retained. This produces
189,792,028 training records. Split allocation, the predefined nablaDFT conformation-test
exception, and deduplication counts are detailed in the Supporting Information.

We then apply different exclusion policies to the benchmark panels. For all 285 CASF
core ligands and the original 23 PLINDER identities, we remove both molecular matches
and a neighborhood of similar molecules. Similarity uses 2,048-bit radius-2 Morgan
fingerprints without chirality and Tanimoto similarity strictly greater than 0.4.
The neighborhood includes similarity to protonation and tautomer variants matching
the benchmark ligands; matching variants of the resulting similar molecules are also
removed. For ref, we remove only exact matches under the normalized identity rule
for the 1,236 ligands before crystal deduplication, without a separate similarity
screen. Here, exact matching is defined by either a normalized InChIKey connectivity
block or a stereochemistry-independent protomer hash, rather than identical SMILES.

All four v2 models use exports from this jointly filtered training collection.
Core and PLINDER therefore exclude the specified similarity neighborhoods, whereas
ref analogues may remain unless removed by the core or PLINDER screen. The filter
removes 2,393,927 records (1.26\%), leaving 187,398,101. The Supporting Information
describes the bounded expansion rule, separate treatment of validation and test data,
and completed training exposure (Table~\ref{tab:qwen-training}).

For the four v2 models, generation metadata record 1,000 requested samples per molecule,
temperature 1.0, and top-$p$ 1.0. Decoded coordinates undergo the same downstream
assessment as the other generators. Failed or incomplete generations reduce the saved
pool; candidate targets and validity denominators are defined below. Sampling seeds
and the distinction between recorded settings and inspected implementation defaults
are documented in the Supporting Information.

An older 0.6B Qwen model with binned coordinates, identified as CASF-dedup PM12 at
step 64,000, is also evaluated in Table~\ref{tab:druglike} and
Figure~\ref{fig:plinder-molecule-tracking}. It is distinct from the four v2 models and
uses the same cleaned PLINDER references. Its training and exclusion history cannot
be established from its name and is not covered by the v2 records. External pretrained
models are evaluated as supplied; their reported training resources are summarized
in Supporting Table S5.

\subsection{Sampling targets and retained ensembles}

For CASF core and ref, we use two sampling targets to reflect different aspects of dataset construction. At the
ChEMBL-count target, each method is assigned the stored conformer count for the corresponding
ChEMBL3D stereoisomer. The subset is drawn without replacement from the available candidate pool and
capped at the pool size. The second target uses a pool of up to 1,000 candidates per molecule. These
are two targets applied to existing pools, rather than independent generation runs at each target.

A candidate is a conformer in the saved pool after the generator's own preparation,
including any method-specific minimization or clash rejection. The target therefore counts
available candidates, not all attempted embeddings, decoded sequences, or optimization attempts.
Both targets are defined before PoseBusters filtering. Rejected conformers are not replaced, so
retained ensemble sizes differ between methods. ChEMBL3D-PB remains the same finite stored
ensemble when generators receive the larger allowance. Candidate selection is reproducible using
fixed seeds; the comparisons do not match retained ensemble sizes or computational cost.
PLINDER uses the supplied generated ensembles and is not included in this two-target comparison.

\subsection{Molecular identity and structural validity}

For the CASF comparison, we retain conformers that pass the specified PoseBusters\cite{buttenschoen2024posebusters} checks for
molecular identity, stereochemistry, and intramolecular geometry. These assess chemical
consistency, bond lengths and angles, steric clashes, planarity, and excessive strain relative to
an internally generated conformer ensemble of the same molecule, not its experimental
bound structure. No protein-contact checks are used. The configured checks and thresholds
are recorded in the Supporting Information. Passing these checks does not guarantee compatibility with a receptor or uniformly favorable
energies under the separate energy analysis.

For some CASF method--entry pairs, every candidate is rejected. At the ChEMBL-count target,
this leaves 92--94 measurable ensembles on core and 1,032--1,044 on ref, depending on the
method. Recovery includes all 94 core or 1,044 ref entries, counting empty, missing, or
unmeasurable outputs as failures. Mean RMSD and diversity summarize entries with defined
measurements; energy summaries additionally exclude undefined energies. Paired diversity and
energy summaries use the same entries for each method. Mean retained counts include zeros.
Mean retained ensemble sizes are reported in Supporting Table S1; measurable energy-pool
counts and energy-calculation completeness are given in Supporting Table S6.

PLINDER coverage, matching distances, and clustering use the full supplied pools. PoseBusters
validity is measured separately and pooled across conformers. For its classical baselines,
atom ordering is matched to the reference topology before validity checking.
The pooled pass rate is the total number of passing conformers divided by the total number
supplied across the 22 molecules; it is not the unweighted mean of molecule-level pass rates.
Mean supplied counts describe the available ensembles before this assessment. Supporting
Figures S5--S6 report per-molecule coverage, precision, and validity on these same pools.
Thus, the two benchmarks use different
filtering conditions, and their absolute performance levels are not directly comparable.

\subsection{Geometric comparison and diversity}

We compare internal conformations using aligned heavy-atom RMSD, allowing equivalent atom mappings
when identifying the best alignment. The archived CASF evaluation falls back to an index-based
mapping if this alignment fails; unresolved comparisons are treated as unmeasurable. The frequency
of this fallback remains to be audited; the unresolved audit and software-version
requirements are listed in the Supporting Information.

Conformers are grouped using greedy clustering in their stored order. Each conformer is assigned to
the nearest existing representative when the aligned RMSD is strictly below the clustering radius;
otherwise, it starts a new cluster. Representatives remain fixed. We
report clustering radii of 0.5 and 1.0 \AA{}. The count depends on the size and ordering of the retained pool and describes
geometric variation rather than occupied energy basins.

To describe how sampling is distributed across clusters, we also calculate the fraction of
retained conformers assigned to the largest cluster at 1.0 \AA{}. This is the largest cluster
population divided by the ensemble size for each molecule, then averaged across molecules. Smaller fractions indicate less concentration in
a single cluster and complement the number of clusters represented in an ensemble. This
statistic is reported for all three panels. Cluster counts also give each molecule equal weight.

\subsection{Recovery of individual bound conformations}

For each CASF entry, we calculate the minimum aligned heavy-atom RMSD between the retained ensemble
and its experimental reference. An entry is recovered when at least one conformer has RMSD $\leq$
0.75 \AA{}. Hit@0.75 is the fraction of all mapped entries satisfying this condition. Table~\ref{tab:count-recovery}
also reports Hit@0.5 at the tighter 0.5 \AA{} cutoff, using the same definition.
Best RMSD in Table~\ref{tab:count-recovery} and Figure~\ref{fig:diversity-recovery} is the
mean of the per-entry minimum RMSDs, rather than an average over all conformers or the single
smallest distance across the panel. Supporting Figure S3 compares each fixed-target
ensemble with ChEMBL3D-PB on the same ligands, counting those recovered only by the
generator and those recovered only by the stored ensemble.

\subsection{Coverage of multiple experimental conformations}

For each molecule, we compare its supplied generated ensemble with all retained experimental
references using aligned heavy-atom RMSD. COV-R is the fraction of references with at least one
generated conformer at RMSD $\leq 0.75$ \AA{}. COV-P is the fraction of generated conformers
with at least one reference within the same threshold. MAT-R averages the distance from each
reference to its nearest generated conformer; MAT-P averages the distance from each generated
conformer to its nearest reference. Each metric is calculated per molecule and then averaged
equally across the 22 molecules. Table~\ref{tab:count-recovery} also reports COV-R at 0.5 \AA{}.
All methods use the same inclusive cutoff convention as CASF recovery.

Tables~\ref{tab:count-recovery} and \ref{tab:druglike} and Figure~\ref{fig:druglike}
use the same nearest-distance records. Every reference and generated
conformer has a finite nearest distance. The threshold curves apply these distances at cutoffs
from 0 to 1.5 \AA{} in increments of 0.005 \AA{}.

COV-P and MAT-P describe proximity to the available observations. Because experimental
references do not exhaust accessible conformational space, an unmatched generated conformer
is not automatically physically inaccessible.

\subsection{Energy analysis}

We evaluate the retained CASF core and ref ChEMBL-count ensembles with MMFF94s in RDKit 2025.09.6, using a common hydrogen
preparation for every method. Existing hydrogens are removed and rebuilt with assigned coordinates, and a single-point
energy is calculated without optimizing either hydrogen or heavy-atom positions. This preserves the conformations used for
clustering and recovery and avoids differences arising from whether a generator supplied
hydrogen coordinates. Saved PoseBusters membership is preserved in both panels.
Energies are reported in kcal mol$^{-1}$, with the nonbonded threshold set to 100
and interfragment interactions ignored. Previously stored energy values are not reused. Calculation settings,
coordinate-preservation checks, and source records are archived with the energy results.

For each molecule and method, we calculate the mean energy and population standard deviation
across conformers. Figure~\ref{fig:energy-dispersion} reports the median of these standard
deviations across molecules, alongside mean cluster counts.
This summary limits the influence of molecules with exceptionally large energy spreads.
Supporting Table S6 also gives the mean of the standard deviations, the median of per-molecule
mean energies, and the median paired difference in mean energy relative to the ChEMBL3D
ensemble of the same molecule. Undefined energies are omitted from the energy moments and counted separately in Supporting
Table S6; all finite values, including extreme values, remain in the summaries.
Energy spread describes dispersion under this force field; it is neither a count of energy
basins nor a binding free energy.

\subsection{Dependence on sampling budget}

To examine how recovery changes with candidate budget, we evaluated each CASF panel at 63
candidate budgets from 10 to 1,000 using the saved fixed-target pools.
Figure~\ref{fig:sampling-option-b1} shows Qwen, LoQI, FlowR, and NExT-Mol. For each entry and budget,
we calculated the exact probability that a uniformly selected subset without replacement would
contain at least one retained conformer within the RMSD cutoff. We used the candidate count and the
number of qualifying conformers to calculate this probability directly, rather than repeatedly
drawing random subsets. Rejected candidates consume budget but cannot produce a hit. Budgets are
capped at the available candidate count. Expected recovery averages these probabilities across
all 94 core or 1,044 ref entries, with empty retained pools contributing zero.

We report expected recovery at a fixed RMSD cutoff of 0.75 \AA{} and, separately, the smallest RMSD
threshold at which expected recovery reaches 80\% at each budget. The latter is found among the
observed RMSD values and is calculated from the average recovery across entries. The stored
ChEMBL3D-PB ensemble supplies a fixed reference in both comparisons. An unattainable
80\% target is left undefined rather than extrapolated. These calculations describe
sampling within the available pools; they do not extrapolate beyond them.

\subsection{Molecular size and flexibility}

We stratify CASF core and ref using descriptors of the reference ligand identities.
Heavy-atom groups are fewer than 20, 20--29, 30--39, and at least 40 atoms. Flexibility
uses RDKit's rotatable-bond count, grouped as 0--3, 4--6, 7--8, and at least 9. The same
reference-derived group assignment is used for every generator. CASF group recovery at the
1,000-candidate target is accompanied by recovered and total counts. Size and flexibility
are analyzed separately; neither analysis adjusts for the other descriptor or establishes an
independent cause of difficulty. Groups with fewer than five entries are marked;
empty groups remain unreported. Supporting Figure S4 cross-tabulates the two
descriptors using the same group boundaries and panel denominators.

\subsection{Qwen model size and coordinate representation}

We compare 0.6B and 1.7B Qwen models with either binned coordinates or FSQ tokens.
Binned coordinates use 256 uniform bins over $-12$ to $12$ \AA{}, with two additional
overflow symbols. FSQ uses 4,096 discrete codes, one per atom plus a molecular prefix.
The binned checkpoints complete training at step 66,057 and the FSQ checkpoints at
step 41,054. Within each representation, the two model sizes share the training export
and one-epoch schedule. All four use a context length of 4,096 tokens and a peak
learning rate of $8\times10^{-4}$. The binned export contains 183,835,937 records,
compared with 187,398,101 for FSQ, and requires more tokens per record.
Thus, the comparison does not isolate representation from training exposure or match
computational cost. Completed training counts are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:qwen-training}. The older step-64,000 model is excluded from this comparison.

All four configurations are evaluated on the same 1,044 CASF ref identities and
22 PLINDER molecules. CASF matching uses the PoseBusters-passing members of the
1,000-candidate pools; PLINDER uses the full supplied pools for matching and assesses validity separately.
Retained ensemble sizes are not equalized. Figure~\ref{fig:qwen-size-representation}
reports mean CASF Best RMSD, PLINDER MAT-R, and pooled PLINDER validity. Its two rows
show the same observations, connecting model sizes within a representation in the
upper row and representations within a size in the lower row.

Supporting Figure S2 compares matching-distance changes for individual molecules.
Representation differences subtract FSQ from binned distances at each model size;
size differences subtract 1.7B from 0.6B distances within each representation.
Positive values indicate closer matching after the change. The reported fraction
of improved molecules counts strictly positive differences, with ties counted as
no improvement. Every molecule remains in the comparison, including Actinonin.

\subsection{Aggregation and reproducibility}

Except for the pooled PLINDER validity rate, ensemble summaries give each molecular
identity equal weight. CASF hit rates retain the full panel denominator, whereas
continuous metrics use entries with defined measurements as specified above. The
selection of ref crystals is fixed before comparing methods. Means, paired changes,
and rankings are calculated from unrounded records; rounding is applied only for
display. ChEMBL3D-PB remains an unranked stored baseline.

The main comparisons are descriptive and do not display confidence intervals.
Differences among the four Qwen configurations are comparisons of the evaluated
checkpoints, without replicated training runs. Source identities, selection manifests,
per-molecule measurements, generation records, and input hashes are archived with
the tables and figures. Historical exports are retained as provenance but do not
supply the current ref, PLINDER, or Qwen summaries.
