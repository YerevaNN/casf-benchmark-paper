\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Evaluation design}

We evaluate how well finite, protein-independent conformer ensembles cover experimentally observed
bound geometries, with the practical aim of selecting a generation procedure for a large
conformational dataset. Each method generates an ensemble for a molecule without receiving a protein
or binding-site structure. The
PLINDER panel assesses coverage of multiple bound structures of the same molecule; CASF core
and ref provide complementary comparisons against individual experimental references and
stored ChEMBL3D ensembles. We examine geometric variation alongside recovery across the three
sets, with energy and sampling-budget analyses on CASF. Recovery measures similarity of the internal ligand geometry after
alignment; it does not establish placement within a binding pocket or recovery of protein--ligand
interactions.

\subsection{Experimental references and the ChEMBL3D comparison}

We compare generated and existing conformer ensembles using CASF-2016,\cite{su2019casf} a curated dataset of 285
protein--ligand crystal complexes. The bound ligand geometries provide experimental references for
assessing whether a protein-independent ensemble includes an observed conformation.

We match CASF ligands to ChEMBL3D by molecular connectivity and stereochemistry, retaining only
conformers of the matching stereoisomer. Molecules must have a usable reference structure, a
corresponding ChEMBL3D topology, and at least one bond eligible for torsional sampling. Selection
is based on molecular identity rather than similarity to the experimental coordinates. These
criteria yield 94 core complexes representing 94 ChEMBL parent compounds. Applying the same
procedure to the larger reference collection, we retain one crystal per stereochemical ligand
identity. When an identity occurs in multiple complexes, a fixed seeded ranking selects one
crystal independently of model outputs and recovery scores. This yields the ref panel of
1,044 crystal complexes with distinct stereochemical ligand identities, representing 1,042
ChEMBL parent compounds. The collections share 17 parent compounds but no complex identifiers. Both collections
are included in the main tables and the diversity--proximity, energy, and sampling figures.

For each molecule, the stored ChEMBL3D ensemble establishes how much of the experimental geometry is
already represented in an existing computed dataset. This comparison distinguishes coverage already
available in the reference resource from coverage that can be added by a generation procedure.
Because the comparison is restricted to molecules represented in ChEMBL3D, its conclusions concern
conformational coverage within this intersection rather than coverage of the entire CASF chemical
space. The analysis permits a deterministic cap of 2,000 stored conformers for the designated large
reference entry, 1tlp, when its available ensemble exceeds that size.

The multiple-reference evaluation uses a cleaned panel of 22 ligand identities derived from an initial 23-molecule selection from PLINDER
release 2024-06/v2,\cite{durairaj2024plinder} an annotated resource of protein--ligand complexes from the Protein Data Bank.
Records in the ligand-per-system table were grouped by full InChIKey so that different stereoisomers
remained separate. Of 51,280 starting identities, 25,392 passed an RDKit-based physicochemical
screen requiring molecular weight 150--650 Da, cLogP $-$1 to 6, and limits on polarity, hydrogen
bonding, flexibility, charge, and elemental composition. The full criteria are given in Supporting
Table S5.

Survivors were ranked by the number of distinct PDB entries, with PLINDER-system count as the
secondary ordering. Distinct PDB entries were preferred because one multimeric structure can
contribute many systems. Manual review then selected recognizable drugs and medicinal-chemistry-like
protein ligands, excluding common crystallization additives, metabolites, cofactors, and other
compounds outside the intended scope. The initial panel was a manually curated
exploratory set, not an official PLINDER split or a purely algorithmic selection. Exact reproduction
uses the saved identities. The molecule list and panel statistics are provided in Supporting
Information.

The cleaned collection excludes bortezomib because of covalent annotations and contains 1,067
reference conformations across 22 molecules, with 8--117 per molecule. The supplied structures
were deduplicated at a symmetry-aware RMSD threshold of 0.05 \AA{} and aligned using a fixed
anchor atom triplet for each molecule. Subsequent stereochemical inspection removed two
chloramphenicol records and one staurosporine record with inverted stereocentres. We evaluate
the retained representatives equally within each molecule, without weighting them by the number
of original records in their duplicate clusters. This reduces repeated-coordinate weighting but
does not define biologically distinct conformational states. Supporting Tables S6--S7 describe
the cleaned coordinate collection; original selection frequencies are archived separately.

\subsection{Conformer generators}

The generators represent different approaches to conformational sampling. The main comparison
contains four classical pipelines, five external learned generators, and one Qwen model.
Supporting Table S8 summarizes their representations, training provenance, initialization,
and processing. These methods differ in training data and inference procedures as well as
architecture, so the comparison evaluates the resulting generation pipelines rather than isolating
any single modeling choice.

RDKit ETKDGv3\cite{wang2020etkdg} generates conformers from the molecular graph using distance geometry and
experimental torsional preferences, with stereochemistry enforced. We compare the generated
coordinates with a second pipeline that adds MMFF94s\cite{halgren1999mmff94s} minimization. The random
torsion baseline instead perturbs eligible torsions in a stored matched geometry, with
displacements sampled uniformly within $\pm120^\circ$, and rejects severe steric clashes. In CASF, its
minimized variant adds MMFF94s optimization and a further clash check. Raw and minimized pipelines
use separately sampled pools, rather than a paired before-and-after experiment. The paired
PLINDER minimization procedure is described below.

The learned methods represent complementary approaches to conformational sampling. Torsional
Diffusion\cite{jing2022torsional} generates conformers by updating torsion angles, while LoQI\cite{nikitin2025loqi} learns low-energy geometries
from ChEMBL3D using stereochemistry-aware coordinate
diffusion. MCF\cite{wang2024mcf} models molecular coordinates as fields over
a graph representation, and NExT-Mol DMT-L\cite{liu2025nextmol} uses a coordinate diffusion transformer; the evaluated
DMT-L adapter does not use the optional MoLlama component. FlowR\cite{cremer2025flowr}
uses flow matching in a ligand-only configuration with the molecular graph held fixed, followed by
force-field cleanup and stereochemistry checks. Their representations,
training resources, and preparation steps are summarized in Supporting Table S8.

Qwen represents autoregressive generation of a molecular coordinate representation conditioned on
molecular SMILES. The archived comparison uses the 1.7-billion-parameter FSQ model at pretraining
step 47,023 for both CASF and the
22-molecule experiment. Its initial training corpus was broadly assembled, and these results do not
establish generalization after exclusion of benchmark molecules. The revised training corpus
excludes exact matches to core94 and similar molecules using a fingerprint-similarity cutoff of
0.4. For CASF ref, exact matches are excluded, without an additional similarity exclusion for
that panel. An additional 0.6-billion-parameter Qwen model with binned coordinates, identified as
CASF-dedup PM12 at step 64,000, is evaluated in Table~\ref{tab:druglike}, Supporting Table S4,
and Figure~\ref{fig:plinder-molecule-tracking}. It is evaluated on the same cleaned references
and retains its supplied conformer pools. The original checkpoint remains in the other comparisons.
The two Qwen runs differ in model size and coordinate representation as well as training data,
so their comparison does not isolate the effect of training exclusions. The new run name alone
does not establish exclusion of PLINDER molecules; the executed exclusion manifest remains
to be documented. External pretrained checkpoints
are evaluated as supplied, with their documented training provenance and any overlap that can be
checked.

\subsection{Sampling targets and retained ensembles}

For CASF core and ref, we use two sampling targets to reflect different aspects of dataset construction. At the
ChEMBL-count target, each method is assigned the stored conformer count for the corresponding
ChEMBL3D stereoisomer. The subset is drawn without replacement from the available candidate pool and
capped at the pool size. The second target uses a pool of up to 1,000 candidates per molecule. These
are two targets applied to existing pools, rather than independent generation runs at each target.

Both targets are defined before PoseBusters filtering. Rejected conformers are not replaced, so
retained ensemble sizes differ between methods. ChEMBL3D-PB remains the same finite stored
ensemble when generators receive the larger allowance. Candidate selection is reproducible using
fixed seeds; the comparisons do not match retained ensemble sizes or computational cost.
PLINDER uses the supplied generated ensembles and is not included in this two-target comparison.

To examine how recovery changes with candidate budget, we evaluated each CASF panel at 63
candidate budgets from 10 to 1,000 using the saved fixed-target pools.
Figure~\ref{fig:sampling-option-b1} shows Qwen, LoQI, FlowR, and NExT-Mol. For each entry and budget,
we calculated the exact probability that a uniformly selected subset without replacement would
contain at least one retained conformer within the RMSD cutoff. We used the candidate count and the
number of qualifying conformers to calculate this probability directly, rather than repeatedly
drawing random subsets. Rejected candidates consume budget but cannot produce a hit. Budgets are
capped at the available candidate count. Expected recovery averages these probabilities across
entries within each panel.

We report expected recovery at a fixed RMSD cutoff of 0.75 \AA{} and, separately, the smallest RMSD
threshold at which expected recovery reaches 80\% at each budget. The latter is found among the
observed RMSD values and is calculated from the average recovery across entries. The stored
ChEMBL3D-PB ensemble supplies a fixed reference in both comparisons. These calculations describe
sampling within the available pools; they do not extrapolate beyond them.

\subsection{Molecular identity and structural validity}

For the CASF comparison, we retain conformers that pass the specified PoseBusters\cite{buttenschoen2024posebusters} checks for
molecular identity, stereochemistry, and intramolecular geometry. These assess chemical
consistency, bond lengths and angles, steric clashes, planarity, and excessive strain relative to
a reference conformer ensemble. No protein-contact checks are
used. Passing these checks does not guarantee compatibility with a receptor or uniformly favorable
energies under the separate energy analysis.

For some CASF method--entry pairs, every candidate is rejected. At the ChEMBL-count target,
this leaves 92--94 measurable ensembles on core and 1,032--1,044 on ref, depending on the
method. Recovery includes all 94 core or 1,044 ref entries, counting empty, missing, or
unmeasurable outputs as failures. Mean RMSD and diversity summarize entries with defined
measurements; energy summaries additionally exclude undefined energies. Paired diversity and
energy summaries use the same entries for each method. Mean retained counts include zeros.
Per-method ensemble counts and energy-calculation completeness are reported in Supporting
Table S9, with per-entry records archived alongside the results.

PLINDER coverage, matching distances, and clustering use the full supplied pools. PoseBusters
validity is measured separately and pooled across conformers. For its classical baselines,
atom ordering is matched to the reference topology before validity checking; raw-baseline
results use the corrected evaluation with this mapping. Thus, the two benchmarks use different
filtering conditions, and their absolute performance levels are not directly comparable.

\subsection{Geometric comparison and diversity}

We compare internal conformations using aligned heavy-atom RMSD, allowing equivalent atom mappings
when identifying the best alignment. The archived CASF evaluation falls back to an index-based
mapping if this alignment fails; unresolved comparisons are treated as unmeasurable. The frequency
of this fallback remains to be audited.

Conformers are grouped using greedy clustering in their stored order. Each conformer is assigned to
the nearest existing representative when the aligned RMSD is strictly below the clustering radius;
otherwise, it starts a new cluster. Representatives remain fixed. We
report clustering radii of 0.5 and 1.0 \AA{} in Table~\ref{tab:diversity}. The joint diversity--RMSD plot also uses 0.5 \AA{};
the larger-ensemble comparison also uses 0.5 \AA{}. The count depends on the size and ordering of the retained pool and describes
geometric variation rather than occupied energy basins.

To describe how sampling is distributed across clusters, we also calculate the fraction of
retained conformers assigned to the largest cluster at 1.0 \AA{}. This is the largest cluster
population divided by the ensemble size for each molecule, then averaged across molecules. Smaller fractions indicate less concentration in
a single cluster and complement the number of clusters represented in an ensemble. This
statistic is reported for all three panels.

\subsection{Energy analysis}

We evaluate the retained CASF core and ref ChEMBL-count ensembles with MMFF94s in RDKit 2025.09.6, using a common hydrogen
preparation for every method. Existing hydrogens are removed and rebuilt with assigned coordinates, and a single-point
energy is calculated without optimizing either hydrogen or heavy-atom positions. This preserves the conformations used for
clustering and recovery and avoids differences arising from whether a generator supplied
hydrogen coordinates. Saved PoseBusters membership is preserved in both panels.
Previously stored energy values are not reused. Calculation settings,
coordinate-preservation checks, and source records are archived with the energy results.

For each molecule and method, we calculate the mean energy and population standard deviation
across conformers. Figure~\ref{fig:energy-dispersion} reports the median of these standard
deviations across molecules, alongside mean cluster counts.
This summary limits the influence of molecules with exceptionally large energy spreads.
Supporting Table S9 also gives the mean of the standard deviations, the median of per-molecule
mean energies, and the median paired difference in mean energy relative to the ChEMBL3D
ensemble of the same molecule. Finite extreme values remain in these summaries.
Energy spread describes dispersion under this force field; it is neither a count of energy
basins nor a binding free energy.

\subsection{Recovery of individual bound conformations}

For each CASF entry, we calculate the minimum aligned heavy-atom RMSD between the retained ensemble
and its experimental reference. An entry is recovered when at least one conformer has RMSD $\leq$
0.75 \AA{}. Hit@0.75 is the fraction of all mapped entries satisfying this condition. Table~\ref{tab:count-recovery}
also reports Hit@0.5 at the tighter 0.5 \AA{} cutoff, using the same definition.
Best RMSD in Table~\ref{tab:count-recovery} and Figure~\ref{fig:diversity-recovery} is the
mean of the per-entry minimum RMSDs, rather than an average over all conformers or the single
smallest distance across the panel.

The fraction of retained conformers close to the reference addresses how frequently a method samples
near that observation, rather than whether it finds at least one match. This conformer-level fraction has not yet been calculated for the final pools and is not included in the current Results tables.

\subsection{Molecular size and flexibility}

We stratify CASF core and ref using descriptors of the reference ligand identities.
Heavy-atom groups are fewer than 20, 20--29, 30--39, and at least 40 atoms. Flexibility
uses RDKit's rotatable-bond count, grouped as 0--3, 4--6, 7--8, and at least 9. The same
reference-derived group assignment is used for every generator. CASF group recovery at the
1,000-candidate target is accompanied by recovered and total counts. Size and flexibility
are analyzed separately; neither analysis adjusts for the other descriptor or establishes an
independent cause of difficulty. Groups with fewer than five entries are marked;
empty groups remain unreported.

\subsection{Coverage of multiple experimental conformations}

The 22-molecule comparison includes RDKit raw and random torsion sampling, each with 1,000
supplied conformers per molecule. RDKit removes the reference coordinates before embedding from the
molecular graph. The torsion baseline instead starts from the first experimental reference in the
original collection and perturbs its rotatable bonds by up to $\pm120^\circ$, rejecting severe clashes.
It therefore has access to a bound geometry and is reported as a reference-seeded baseline.
The raw baselines undergo no energy minimization. Their minimized counterparts optimize the
same conformers individually with MMFF94s after rebuilding hydrogens, for up to 500 iterations.
Hydrogens are then removed. All conformers of the 22 included molecules are retained, including
those that reach the iteration limit without convergence; no replacement sampling or additional
clash filtering is applied. Both torsion variants retain the reference-seeded initialization and are marked with
$\dagger$ in the displays. The four classical pools are evaluated against the same cleaned references. This paired comparison differs from the
separately sampled raw and minimized CASF pools.

For each molecule, we compare its supplied generated ensemble with all retained experimental
references using aligned heavy-atom RMSD. COV-R is the fraction of references with at least one
generated conformer at RMSD $\leq 0.75$ \AA{}. COV-P is the fraction of generated conformers
with at least one reference within the same threshold. MAT-R averages the distance from each
reference to its nearest generated conformer; MAT-P averages the distance from each generated
conformer to its nearest reference. Each metric is calculated per molecule and then averaged
equally across the 22 molecules. Table~\ref{tab:count-recovery} also reports COV-R at 0.5 \AA{}.
All methods use the same inclusive cutoff convention as CASF recovery.

Tables~\ref{tab:count-recovery} and \ref{tab:druglike}, Supporting Table S4, and
Figure~\ref{fig:druglike} use the same refreshed distances. Every reference and generated
conformer has a finite nearest distance. The threshold curves apply these distances at cutoffs
from 0 to 1.5 \AA{} in increments of 0.005 \AA{}.

Figure~\ref{fig:plinder-molecule-tracking} connects the per-molecule MAT-R and MAT-P values
across methods. Colored trajectories identify the union of molecules with the largest value
for any displayed method in either metric; all other molecules are shown in grey. This
display uses the two raw classical baselines, the five external learned methods, and both
Qwen variants.

Experimental references do not exhaust accessible conformational space, so an unmatched
generated conformer is not automatically physically inaccessible.

\subsection{Aggregation, uncertainty, and reproducibility}

The CASF tables and figures use the corrected findings archived on 24 September 2026 and the
size-stratum export from 28 September. Ref summaries use the frozen 1,044-crystal selection
published on 30 October 2026. The retained per-crystal measurements and common-protocol energy
calculations are unchanged; recovery, diversity, energy summaries, size strata, and sampling
curves are aggregated over this selection. The selection manifest and seed are archived with
the paper data. PLINDER uses the cleaned reference collection and refreshed
evaluations from 20 October 2026, supplemented by the finalized minimized classical runs
and corrected raw-baseline validity checks from 23 October, and the additional Qwen 0.6B
CASF-dedup evaluation from 26 October. The same ten CASF pipelines and the stored ChEMBL3D-PB
ensemble are used throughout, and the 22-molecule comparison uses the five external learned
generators and the same Qwen checkpoint, with four classical baselines in
Tables~\ref{tab:diversity}, \ref{tab:count-recovery}, and \ref{tab:druglike} and Figure~\ref{fig:druglike}.
Table~\ref{tab:druglike} and Supporting Table S4 additionally include the Qwen 0.6B variant.
Main displays are descriptive and omit uncertainty intervals; archived bootstrap intervals
are shown only in the review appendix. Bold and underlining in the tables identify the best
and second-best distinct unrounded values among generated methods, with ties sharing emphasis.
ChEMBL3D-PB and supplied pool sizes are unranked.
