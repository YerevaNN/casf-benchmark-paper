\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Evaluation design and reference panels}

Each method generates conformers from a ligand identity without receiving a protein
or binding-site structure. PLINDER assesses coverage of multiple observed bound
conformations; CASF core and ref compare ensembles with individual experimental
references and stored ChEMBL3D\cite{nikitin2025loqi} conformers. We measure geometric
diversity and experimental recovery in all three panels, with energy and
sampling-budget analyses on CASF. Recovery concerns internal ligand geometry after
alignment, not placement within a binding pocket.

The core panel contains 94 of the 285 protein--ligand complexes in
CASF-2016.\cite{su2019casf} Ligands are matched to ChEMBL3D by canonical heavy-atom
isomeric SMILES, with coordinate-based stereochemical checks. We require a readable
reference, a matching ChEMBL3D topology, and a bond eligible for torsional sampling.
Applying the same criteria to a separately supplied ref archive, followed by selecting
one crystal per ligand identity, yields 1,044 complexes. Selection is independent of
model outputs. The comparison therefore concerns molecules represented in ChEMBL3D,
rather than the full CASF chemical space. Supporting Table~\ref{tab:selection-counts}
and the selection appendix give the exclusion counts, identity rules, and unresolved
source provenance.

The multiple-reference panel derives from PLINDER release 2024-06/v2,\cite{durairaj2024plinder}
an annotated collection of protein--ligand complexes from the Protein Data Bank.
Physicochemical screening, ranking by distinct PDB entries, and manual review produced
an exploratory shortlist of 23 ligands. After excluding bortezomib because of covalent
annotations and removing three reference records with inverted stereochemistry, the
cleaned panel contains 22 molecules and 1,067 reference conformations. The supplied
references were deduplicated at 0.05 \AA{} symmetry-aware RMSD. Retained representatives
receive equal weight within each molecule; they are not assumed to define distinct
biological states. This is a manually curated panel, not a random sample or an official
PLINDER split. Supporting Tables S2--S4 and the selection appendix provide the frozen
identities, screening criteria, coordinate counts, and outstanding cleaning details.

\subsection{Conformer generators}

The main comparison includes four classical pipelines, five external learned generators,
and Qwen. RDKit ETKDGv3\cite{wang2020etkdg} embeds molecular graphs using distance geometry
and experimental torsional preferences. Random torsion sampling perturbs eligible
bonds and rejects severe clashes. Both are evaluated with and without
MMFF94s\cite{halgren1999mmff94s} minimization. CASF torsion sampling starts from matched
ChEMBL3D topology coordinates; PLINDER torsion sampling starts from independently
embedded structures. Neither uses the experimental reference coordinates. Raw and
minimized PLINDER pools are paired, whereas the CASF pools are sampled separately.

Torsional Diffusion\cite{jing2022torsional} updates torsion angles;
LoQI\cite{nikitin2025loqi} learns low-energy geometries from ChEMBL3D through coordinate
diffusion; MCF\cite{wang2024mcf} models coordinates as fields over a graph; and
NExT-Mol DMT-L\cite{liu2025nextmol} uses a coordinate diffusion transformer without its
optional MoLlama component. FlowR\cite{cremer2025flowr} uses ligand-only flow matching
with the molecular graph fixed, followed by force-field cleanup and stereochemical
checks. Supporting Table S5 and the evaluation appendix document preparation and
sampling settings. The comparison evaluates complete generation pipelines, which
differ in training data and processing as well as architecture.

Qwen generates a coordinate-enriched sequence from a SMILES prompt. The primary model
initializes from Qwen3-1.7B-Base\cite{yang2025qwen3} and uses CoordToken,\cite{geikyan2026coordtoken}
which represents geometry through finite scalar quantization (FSQ), with one code per
atom and one molecular code. Its decoder reconstructs coordinates from these codes
and the molecular graph.

\subsection{Training and benchmark exclusions}

CoordToken is trained to reconstruct centered heavy-atom coordinates from molecular
SMILES and discrete codes. We use a separate tokenizer trained for two epochs on the
filtered conformer collection, rather than the full-corpus checkpoint reported in its
paper. Qwen then learns the molecular sequences by next-token prediction while the
geometry tokenizer remains fixed. The four v2 models complete one epoch without
subsequent task-specific fine-tuning.

The training collection combines computed conformers and ligand structures from protein
complexes. Source splits are reconciled by molecular identity, with a predefined
conformation-test exception, and exact duplicate coordinate-enriched strings are removed.
Benchmark exclusions then remove normalized molecular matches and specified similarity
neighborhoods around all 285 CASF core ligands and the original 23 PLINDER identities.
Neighborhoods use ECFP4 Tanimoto similarity strictly greater than 0.4, including matching
protonation and tautomer variants. Ref adds normalized identity exclusions only, without
a separate similarity screen. All four v2 models use exports from this jointly filtered
collection; some ref analogues may also be removed by the core or PLINDER exclusions.
The training appendix defines normalization, neighborhood expansion, split handling,
removal counts, tokenizer development data, and completed training exposure
(Table~\ref{tab:qwen-training}).

The main PLINDER comparison also includes an older 0.6B Qwen model whose training
history remains unresolved; the v2 exclusion records do not establish its provenance.
External pretrained models are evaluated as supplied. Outstanding overlap audits and
run-specific settings are listed in the appendix, rather than assuming equivalent
training exclusions across methods.

\subsection{Sampling targets and structural validity}

For CASF, the ChEMBL-count target draws candidates without replacement up to the stored
conformer count for the matching stereoisomer; the larger target uses up to 1,000
candidates per molecule. Both select from existing pools after each generator's own
preparation. They count available candidates, not all attempted generations, and are
defined before PoseBusters\cite{buttenschoen2024posebusters} filtering. Rejected conformers
are not replaced. ChEMBL3D-PB remains the same finite stored ensemble at either target.
Retained counts and computational cost are not equalized.

CASF ensembles retain conformers passing the specified molecular identity,
stereochemistry, and intramolecular geometry checks. These include bond geometry,
clashes, planarity, and strain relative to internally generated conformers; no receptor
contact tests are used. Recovery includes every panel entry, counting empty, missing,
or unmeasurable outputs as failures. Continuous metrics use entries with defined
measurements; mean retained counts include zeros.

PLINDER coverage and clustering use the full supplied pools, with PoseBusters validity
assessed separately. Its pass rate is pooled across all supplied conformers, rather
than averaged across molecules. The panels therefore have different filtering
conditions, and their absolute performance levels are not directly comparable.
Supporting Table S1 and the evaluation appendix give retained counts, validity
settings, sampling procedures, and failure handling.

\subsection{Geometric diversity and experimental recovery}

We compare conformations by aligned heavy-atom RMSD, allowing equivalent atom mappings.
The archived CASF evaluation attempts an index-based mapping if symmetry-aware alignment
fails; the frequency of this fallback has not been quantified. Unresolved comparisons
are unmeasurable.
Greedy clustering processes conformers in saved order, assigning each to its nearest
fixed representative when RMSD is strictly below the radius, or starting a new cluster
otherwise. We report cluster counts at 0.5 and 1.0 \AA{} and the fraction of conformers
in the largest cluster at 1.0 \AA{}, averaging each measure equally across molecules.
Counts depend on pool size and order and do not measure occupied energy basins.

For CASF, Hit@0.75 is the fraction of all panel entries with at least one retained
conformer within 0.75 \AA{} of the experimental reference. Hit@0.5 uses the tighter
0.5 \AA{} cutoff. Best RMSD is the mean of the per-entry minimum distances, not the
mean distance over all conformers or the single smallest distance in the panel.

For PLINDER, COV-R is the fraction of experimental references with a generated
conformer within 0.75 \AA{}; COV-P is the fraction of generated conformers with a
reference within the same cutoff. MAT-R averages the distance from each reference
to its nearest generated conformer, while MAT-P averages distances in the reverse
direction. Metrics are calculated per molecule and averaged equally across the 22
molecules. We also report COV-R at 0.5 \AA{} and threshold curves. Recovery cutoffs
are inclusive in both panels. All reported PLINDER nearest distances are finite.
Because experimental references are incomplete, unmatched generated conformers are
not automatically physically inaccessible.

\subsection{Energy and sampling-budget analyses}

We calculate MMFF94s single-point energies for the retained CASF ChEMBL-count ensembles.
Existing hydrogens are removed and rebuilt consistently across methods; neither
hydrogen nor heavy-atom positions are optimized. For each molecule, we calculate the
mean energy and population standard deviation. The principal dispersion summary is
the median standard deviation across molecules, limiting the influence of extreme
spreads. Undefined energies are omitted and counted separately; all finite values
are retained. Supporting Table S6 and the evaluation appendix give additional
summaries and calculation settings. These energies do not represent binding free
energies.

Sampling curves describe uniformly selected subsets of the saved CASF candidate pools.
For each budget, we calculate the exact probability of including at least one qualifying
conformer, without repeatedly drawing subsets. Rejected candidates consume budget but
cannot produce hits, and empty retained pools contribute zero. We report expected
recovery at 0.75 \AA{} and the smallest matching tolerance reaching 80\% expected
recovery. ChEMBL3D-PB supplies a fixed reference. Budgets are capped at available
candidates; unattainable targets remain undefined. The appendix records the budget
grid and calculation procedure. No extrapolation beyond the saved pools is made.

\subsection{Molecular complexity and Qwen comparisons}

We stratify CASF recovery at 1,000 candidates by reference-ligand heavy-atom count and
rotatable-bond count, using the same assignments for all generators. Group boundaries,
counts, and treatment of sparse groups are reported with Figure~\ref{fig:size}
and in the appendix. Size and flexibility are analyzed separately, without adjusting
for their association.

The size and representation comparison evaluates 0.6B and 1.7B Qwen models with binned
coordinates or FSQ tokens on the same ref and PLINDER panels. CASF uses PoseBusters-passing
members of the 1,000-candidate pools; PLINDER uses full supplied pools and assesses
validity separately. The older 0.6B model is excluded from this comparison. Within each
representation, both sizes share a training export and one-epoch schedule. Binned and
FSQ exports differ in record count and tokens per record, so the comparison does not
isolate representation from training exposure or match computational cost. The appendix
provides checkpoint identities, training settings, and molecule-level display definitions.

\subsection{Aggregation and reproducibility}

Except for pooled PLINDER validity, ensemble summaries give each molecule equal weight.
CASF hit rates retain the full panel denominator, while continuous metrics use defined
measurements. Paired diversity and energy summaries use the same entries for each
method. Means, paired changes, and rankings use unrounded records; ChEMBL3D-PB remains
an unranked baseline. The comparisons are descriptive, without displayed confidence
intervals or replicated Qwen training runs. Source manifests, per-molecule measurements,
and input hashes accompany the tables and figures. The appendix retains detailed
procedures and identifies incomplete execution records and provenance.
