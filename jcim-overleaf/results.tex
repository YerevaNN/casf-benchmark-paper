\subsection{Recovery gains with increased sampling}

Recovery in the core cohort of 94 ligands increased with the candidate
allowance for every generator (Table~\ref{tab:recovery}). At the principal
cutoff of 0.75~\AA{}, the pretrained Qwen 1.7B FSQ model had the highest
observed recovery at both targets, increasing from 80.9\% at the
ChEMBL-count target to 91.5\% at 1,000 candidates. Torsional Diffusion
ranked second at the larger target, whereas NExT-Mol ranked second among
the generators at the smaller target. ChEMBL3D-PB was evaluated as the
same stored ensemble in both comparisons.

At the ChEMBL-count target, Qwen and ChEMBL3D-PB each retained approximately
80 conformers per ligand on average. Their recovery difference was 2.1 percentage
points (95\% confidence interval, $-4.3$ to 8.5), with no resolved advantage
for Qwen. At the larger target, Qwen retained approximately 973 conformers
and recovered every ligand recovered by ChEMBL3D-PB, together with 12
additional ligands. The resulting advantage of 12.8 percentage points
(95\% confidence interval, 6.4--20.2) was therefore specific to the larger
sampling allowance (Figure~\ref{fig:recovery}B).

In the larger reference cohort, Qwen recovered fewer ligands than
ChEMBL3D-PB at the smaller target and more at 1,000 candidates (Table~S2).
The advantage at the larger target persisted after excluding compounds
shared with core. An advantage over ChEMBL3D-PB was resolved only at the
larger sampling allowance. Candidate targets were defined
before filtering, so these
comparisons do not establish rankings at identical retained ensemble sizes
or computational cost.

\subsection{Recovery rankings at stricter RMSD cutoffs}

LoQI exceeded Qwen in core recovery when closer structural agreement was
required at the target of 1,000 candidates. At a cutoff of 0.5~\AA{},
recovery was 83.0\% for LoQI and 78.7\% for Qwen, reversing their ordering
at the principal cutoff. LoQI also had higher recovery at 0.25~\AA{}
(Figure~\ref{fig:recovery}A).

Qwen had a lower mean minimum RMSD than LoQI and ChEMBL3D-PB at the fixed
target, but the three methods had similar mean ensemble median RMSDs
(Table~S1). Its higher recovery at 0.75~\AA{} consequently reflected the
closest conformers in each ensemble, without a corresponding reduction
in the distance of a typical conformer from the experimental geometry.

\subsection{Geometric diversity and experimental recovery}

Greater geometric diversity did not consistently correspond to higher
recovery. At the target of 1,000 candidates and a clustering radius of
1.0~\AA{}, FlowR produced more clusters than Qwen on core (114.4 versus
72.8 per ligand) but recovered fewer experimental conformations. Torsion
perturbation produced a cluster count close to Qwen's with lower recovery,
while LoQI matched FlowR's recovery with substantially fewer clusters
(Figure~\ref{fig:diversity-recovery}B).

In the larger reference cohort, FlowR also produced more clusters than
Qwen and LoQI while recovering fewer ligands than either. Cluster counts
decreased as the clustering radius
increased (Figure~\ref{fig:diversity-recovery}A), reflecting the resolution
at which conformers were considered distinct. Because these counts also
depend on retained ensemble size and clustering order, they quantify
geometric variation within the sampled pool rather than the number of
biologically accessible states.

\subsection{Lower recovery for larger and more flexible ligands}

At the target of 1,000 candidates, Qwen, LoQI, and FlowR recovered all core
ligands with fewer than 20 heavy atoms, but their performance diverged for
larger molecules (Table~\ref{tab:size}; Figure~\ref{fig:size}). Among the
12 ligands with 30--39 heavy atoms, Qwen recovered twice the fraction
recovered by FlowR (66.7\% versus 33.3\%), with LoQI and ChEMBL3D-PB
between them. The core cohort contained only two ligands with at least
40 heavy atoms, precluding a reliable comparison in that group.

The larger reference cohort showed the same ordering among these methods
for ligands with 30--39 heavy atoms. Recovery remained poor for the largest
molecules: Qwen recovered only one-third of the 24 entries with at
least 40 heavy atoms. Recovery also declined with increasing numbers of
rotatable bonds, and Qwen's largest absolute advantage over ChEMBL3D-PB
occurred among ligands with 7--8 rotatable bonds rather than in the most
flexible group (Figure~\ref{fig:size}). Size and flexibility were not
adjusted for one another, so neither association establishes an independent
cause of the decline.

\subsection{Reference coverage and sample precision}

Across the separate panel of 23 drug molecules, Qwen and LoQI each covered
approximately 91\% of the available experimental conformations
(Table~\ref{tab:druglike}). The paired coverage difference for Qwen
relative to LoQI was unresolved (95\% confidence interval, $-9.1$ to
8.7 percentage points), whereas a
larger fraction of LoQI's generated conformers lay near an experimental
reference. Qwen's precision was lower by 12.2 percentage points
(95\% confidence interval, 4.7--19.8). Similar mean reference coverage
therefore coincided with different concentrations of samples near the
observed geometries (Figure~\ref{fig:druglike}).

\input{tables/druglike}

The similarity in mean coverage did not extend to individual molecules:
Qwen covered substantially more imatinib references, while LoQI covered
substantially more actinonin references (Figure~\ref{fig:druglike}B).
These results describe the supplied pools, which differed in validity
filtering and the treatment of failed RMSD calculations (Methods).
Moreover, the experimental records need not represent distinct states or
exhaust the molecule's accessible conformations; low precision relative
to these records does not establish that unmatched conformers are
physically inaccessible.
