We evaluate whether protein-independent conformer ensembles represent the bound geometries
observed for a molecule across experimental structures. The PLINDER\cite{durairaj2024plinder} panel provides multiple
PDB-derived references for each molecule, allowing recovery of observed conformations to be
distinguished from the concentration of generated samples near them.
The CASF core and ref panels complement this comparison with one reference per complex and a
stored ChEMBL3D ensemble. We examine geometric variation and experimental recovery across all
three sets, then assess sampling effects within CASF and sample precision within PLINDER.

Table~\ref{tab:diversity} compares cluster counts at two geometric resolutions. Across the
three sets, FlowR, Qwen, and raw torsion sampling generally produce more clusters than LoQI
and the minimized CASF baselines. Their exact ordering depends on the dataset and clustering
radius: FlowR has the most clusters on PLINDER at both radii, while Qwen leads the finer-radius
CASF comparisons.

\input{\manuscriptroot tables/diversity}

Cluster occupancy distinguishes the number of represented shapes from how evenly
they are sampled. FlowR has the smallest mean largest-cluster fraction in all three panels,
whereas LoQI and the minimized CASF torsion baseline concentrate more conformers in one cluster.
The stored ChEMBL3D ensembles combine relatively few clusters with less concentration
than those two methods. Cluster counts and occupancy thus describe different aspects of
geometric variation; neither establishes proximity to the observed bound conformations.

Table~\ref{tab:count-recovery} compares proximity to the experimental conformations through
reference coverage and matching distance.

\input{\manuscriptroot tables/count-recovery}

Qwen has the highest PLINDER coverage at the primary cutoff and the lowest mean matching
distance, while LoQI covers more references at the tighter tolerance. The two CASF
panels show a related dependence on the metric: among generated methods, LoQI leads at the
tighter cutoff, whereas Qwen has the highest recovery at the primary cutoff and the lowest
Best RMSD. The stored ChEMBL3D ensembles remain competitive; on ref, their primary-cutoff
recovery exceeds that of every generator at the ChEMBL-count target. A method's ranking
therefore depends on whether the objective is frequent close matches or a smaller average
distance to the reference.

Minimizing the existing PLINDER classical pools reduces geometric diversity and increases
coverage at the primary cutoff, but coverage falls at the tighter cutoff. The smaller
cluster counts therefore do not imply a uniform change in recovery of bound geometries.

Broad geometric variation alone does not explain these recovery patterns. On PLINDER,
FlowR produces the most clusters but covers fewer references than LoQI, whose ensembles
contain substantially fewer clusters (Figure~\ref{fig:druglike}A--B). The coverage and precision
curves separate recovery of the observed set from concentration near it.

The CASF comparisons show a related distinction (Figure~\ref{fig:diversity-recovery}).
Raw torsion sampling and FlowR approach Qwen's cluster count but have higher Best RMSD,
while LoQI and ChEMBL3D approach the references with fewer clusters. Diversity is consequently
an incomplete indicator of recovery of experimental conformations.

\diversityfigure

Energy variation also differs among geometrically diverse ensembles. On both CASF panels,
Qwen and FlowR have narrower typical energy distributions than raw torsion sampling and
Torsional Diffusion, while LoQI, the minimized baselines, and ChEMBL3D have narrower
distributions still (Figure~\ref{fig:energy-dispersion}). Under the common MMFF94s calculation,
extreme ensembles make mean energy spreads much larger than medians for several methods.
LoQI on ref illustrates that a narrow typical distribution can coexist with pronounced
outliers (Supporting Table S9). Geometric breadth and energy dispersion therefore describe
different properties of an ensemble.

\energyfigure

The CASF panels allow a separate comparison of candidate targets because each molecule has
a corresponding stored ChEMBL3D ensemble. Increasing the target improves recovery and
reduces Best RMSD for every evaluated generator in both core and ref
(Table~\ref{tab:recovery}). Cluster counts also increase, but their ordering does not match
the recovery ordering. Qwen retains the highest primary-cutoff recovery among generators,
while FlowR produces the most clusters; raw torsion sampling is also highly diverse.
Additional sampling therefore expands geometric variation and experimental recovery to
different degrees across methods.

Aggregate recovery can conceal differences in molecular difficulty. Across the three sets,
recovery generally declines for larger or more flexible ligands, although the methods differ
in how strongly performance falls (Figure~\ref{fig:size}). CASF ref extends the comparison
to more populated strata; PLINDER shows reduced coverage of multiple experimental references
in its larger and more flexible groups. The sparse PLINDER and core groups cannot support
stable method rankings. These patterns caution against interpreting a panel-wide average
as uniform coverage of the chemistry needed for a conformational resource.

\sizefigure

PLINDER's multiple experimental references also distinguish coverage from the concentration
of generated conformers near the observed set. Table~\ref{tab:druglike} reports sample
precision alongside reference coverage, matching distances, and separately measured validity.
Qwen covers the most references at the primary cutoff, whereas minimized torsion places the
largest fraction of samples near an observed geometry. Among the learned generators, LoQI
has the highest precision. Reference coverage and sample precision therefore favor different
methods.

\input{\manuscriptroot tables/druglike}

The threshold curves reinforce this distinction (Figure~\ref{fig:druglike}). LoQI has higher
coverage at 0.5 \AA{}, while Qwen has higher coverage at 1.0 \AA{};
LoQI maintains higher sample precision than Qwen at both and at the primary cutoff.

Together, the three evaluation sets support selecting generators by experimental coverage
and sample concentration, with geometric diversity and energetic dispersion as complementary
descriptors. Qwen remains a candidate for constructing a larger conformational resource,
subject to confirmation after retraining. The resulting resource
will require its own characterization of molecular coverage, validity, diversity, and
energy distributions. Its value for downstream structure-based applications remains to be
evaluated separately.
