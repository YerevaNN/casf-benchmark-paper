We evaluate whether protein-independent conformer ensembles represent the bound geometries
observed for a molecule across experimental structures. The PLINDER panel provides multiple
PDB-derived references for each molecule, allowing recovery of observed conformations to be
distinguished from the concentration of generated samples near them.\cite{durairaj2024plinder}
The CASF core and ref panels complement this comparison with one reference per complex and a
stored ChEMBL3D ensemble. We examine geometric variation and experimental recovery across all
three sets, then assess sampling effects within CASF and sample precision within PLINDER.

The current results use the archived PLINDER-23 panel and the original Qwen checkpoint.
They precede the removal of a covalently annotated PLINDER molecule and evaluation after the
updated training exclusions. PLINDER measurements use supplied ensembles without a common
PoseBusters filter, whereas CASF measurements use filtered ensembles. Comparisons therefore
describe performance within each evaluation setting rather than a common level of difficulty
across datasets.

Table~\ref{tab:diversity} compares cluster counts at two geometric resolutions. Across the
three sets, FlowR, Qwen, and raw torsion sampling generally produce more clusters than LoQI
and the minimized CASF baselines. Their exact ordering depends on the dataset and clustering
radius: FlowR has the most clusters on PLINDER at both radii, while Qwen leads the finer-radius
CASF comparisons. This broad separation persists despite differences in ensemble size and
filtering, although absolute counts across datasets are not directly comparable.

For CASF, cluster occupancy distinguishes the number of represented shapes from how evenly
they are sampled. FlowR has the smallest mean largest-cluster fraction in both panels,
whereas LoQI and minimized torsion sampling concentrate more of their conformers in one
cluster. The stored ChEMBL3D ensembles combine relatively few clusters with less concentration
than those two methods. Cluster counts and occupancy thus describe different aspects of
geometric variation; neither establishes proximity to the observed bound conformations.

Table~\ref{tab:count-recovery} measures this proximity using reference coverage and matching
distance. On PLINDER, COV-R records the fraction of experimental references recovered for
each molecule, while MAT-R averages the distance from each reference to its closest generated
conformer. The CASF sections report the corresponding single-reference outcomes, Hit and
Best RMSD, with their respective missing-output conventions.

LoQI and Qwen have similar PLINDER coverage at the primary cutoff. LoQI covers more references
at the tighter tolerance, while Qwen has the lowest mean matching distance. The two CASF
panels show a related dependence on the metric: among generated methods, LoQI leads at the
tighter cutoff, whereas Qwen has the highest recovery at the primary cutoff and the lowest
Best RMSD. The stored ChEMBL3D ensembles remain competitive; on ref, their primary-cutoff
recovery exceeds that of every generator at the ChEMBL-count target. A method's ranking
therefore depends on whether the objective is frequent close matches or a smaller average
distance to the reference.

Broad geometric variation alone does not explain these recovery patterns. On PLINDER,
FlowR produces the most clusters but covers fewer references than LoQI, whose ensembles
contain substantially fewer clusters. The core comparison in Figure~\ref{fig:diversity-recovery}
shows a similar distinction: raw torsion sampling and FlowR approach Qwen's cluster count
but have higher Best RMSD, while LoQI and ChEMBL3D approach the references with fewer
clusters. Diversity is consequently an incomplete indicator of recovery of experimental
conformations, rather than a sufficient criterion for selecting a generator.

Energy variation also differs among geometrically diverse ensembles. On CASF core,
Qwen and FlowR have narrower typical energy distributions than raw torsion sampling and
Torsional Diffusion, while LoQI, the minimized baselines, and ChEMBL3D have narrower
distributions still (Figure~\ref{fig:energy-dispersion}). These comparisons use a common
MMFF94s calculation without coordinate optimization. Exceptional molecules strongly affect
the mean energy spread, particularly for Torsional Diffusion; the median better represents
the typical ensemble. Supporting Table S9 reports both summaries and the energy levels.
A narrower energy distribution does not establish lower absolute energy or thermodynamic
stability.

The CASF panels allow a separate comparison of candidate targets because each molecule has
a corresponding stored ChEMBL3D ensemble. Increasing the target improves recovery and
reduces Best RMSD for every evaluated generator in both core and ref
(Table~\ref{tab:recovery}). Cluster counts also increase, but their ordering does not match
the recovery ordering. Qwen retains the highest primary-cutoff recovery among generators,
while FlowR produces the most clusters; raw torsion sampling is also highly diverse.
Additional sampling therefore expands geometric variation and experimental recovery to
different degrees across methods.

The core endpoints are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:recovery}. At the larger target, several
generators recover references missed by the stored ChEMBL3D ensembles. The stored comparison
remains finite and unchanged, so this does not establish what ChEMBL3D would achieve with
an equally enlarged pool. Candidate targets also yield different retained counts and do
not imply equal computational costs.

Aggregate recovery can conceal differences in molecular difficulty. The core size and
flexibility strata identify uneven recovery across chemical groups, but the largest and
most flexible groups contain too few entries to support stable method rankings
(Figure~\ref{fig:size}). These results caution against using a panel-wide average as evidence
of uniform coverage of the chemistry needed for a conformational resource.

\sizefigure

PLINDER's multiple experimental references also distinguish coverage from the concentration
of generated conformers near the observed set. Table~\ref{tab:druglike} reports sample
precision alongside reference coverage, matching distances, and separately measured validity.
LoQI and Qwen attain similar reference coverage, but LoQI places a larger fraction of its
generated conformers near an observed geometry. Thus, ensembles that cover similar fractions
of the reference set can distribute their remaining samples differently.

\input{\manuscriptroot tables/druglike}

The threshold curves reinforce this distinction (Figure~\ref{fig:druglike}). LoQI has higher
coverage at 0.5 \AA{}, while Qwen has higher coverage at 1.0 \AA{};
LoQI maintains higher sample precision at both and at the primary cutoff. These observations
describe the supplied pools. Common validity filtering and consistent RMSD-failure handling
remain necessary for a definitive comparison. Experimental references are also incomplete,
so an unmatched conformer cannot automatically be classified as physically inaccessible
or irrelevant to binding.

Together, the three evaluation sets support selecting generators by experimental coverage
and sample concentration, with geometric diversity and energetic dispersion as complementary
descriptors. Qwen remains a candidate for constructing a larger conformational resource,
subject to confirmation after retraining and benchmark curation. The resulting resource
will require its own characterization of molecular coverage, validity, diversity, and
energy distributions. Its value for downstream structure-based applications remains to be
evaluated separately.
