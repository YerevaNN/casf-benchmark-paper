\subsection{Recovery under validity and sampling constraints}

The ten generation pipelines differed in how often they produced a
PoseBusters-passing conformer within 0.75~\AA{} of the bound geometry across
the 94 core ligands (Table~\ref{tab:recovery}; Figure~\ref{fig:recovery}).
At the 1,000-candidate target, recovery ranged from 78.7\% to 91.5\%.
The six learned generators each recovered more ligands than the tested
classical pipelines at this target. Qwen had the highest observed recovery
in the selected panel, followed by Torsional Diffusion, with only two
ligands separating them. Qwen exceeded RDKit raw by 10.6 percentage points
(95\% confidence interval, 4.3--18.1), whereas its difference from LoQI
was unresolved (5.3 percentage points; 95\% confidence interval,
$-1.1$ to 11.7). These comparisons support an advantage over the tested
RDKit configuration without establishing a unique best learned generator.

Recovery increased with the candidate allowance for every generator
(Figure~\ref{fig:recovery}).
At the ChEMBL-count target, Qwen and the stored ChEMBL3D-PB ensemble
each retained approximately 80 conformers per ligand. Their recovery
difference was 2.1 percentage points (95\% confidence interval,
$-4.3$ to 8.5), with no resolved advantage for Qwen. At the larger target,
Qwen retained approximately 973 conformers and recovered every ligand
recovered by ChEMBL3D-PB, together with 12 additional ligands. Its advantage
over that stored ensemble was 12.8 percentage points
(95\% confidence interval, 6.4--20.2).

The candidate allowance did not determine the retained ensemble size:
filtering and available outputs left different numbers of conformers for
different methods (Table~\ref{tab:recovery}). ChEMBL3D-PB remained the
same finite stored ensemble in both comparisons. These endpoints therefore
compare the evaluated pipelines at specified candidate targets, rather
than at identical retained counts or computational cost.

Qwen's lower mean minimum RMSD was not accompanied by a lower mean
ensemble median RMSD than LoQI or ChEMBL3D-PB (Table~S1). Its higher
recovery thus concerned the closest members of the pool without evidence
that the typical conformer was closer to the bound geometry. The larger
reference cohort is reported separately in Table~S2.

\subsection{Geometric diversity does not determine recovery}

Greater geometric diversity did not consistently correspond to better
recovery on core. At the 1,000-candidate target and a clustering radius
of 1.0~\AA{}, FlowR produced more clusters than Qwen
(114.4 versus 72.8 per ligand) but recovered fewer bound geometries.
Torsion perturbation produced a cluster count close to Qwen's with lower
recovery, while LoQI matched FlowR's recovery with substantially fewer
clusters (Figure~\ref{fig:diversity-recovery}).

Cluster count therefore did not provide a sufficient criterion for
selecting a generator that recovered the experimental geometry.
The counts also depend on retained ensemble size and clustering order;
they describe geometric variation in the sampled pool rather than the
number of biologically accessible states.

\subsection{Recovery depends on ligand size and flexibility}

Recovery was generally lower for larger core ligands, although the
relative performance of the generators varied between groups
(Figure~\ref{fig:size}A). Among the 12 ligands with 30--39 heavy atoms,
Qwen recovered eight and FlowR recovered four. Only two core ligands
had at least 40 heavy atoms, so their outcomes cannot establish a
reliable ranking for large molecules. Size-stratum values for the
selected comparison methods in both cohorts are provided in Table~S3.

The flexibility groups also exposed differences hidden by the overall
ranking (Figure~\ref{fig:size}B). Qwen and Torsional Diffusion each
recovered 33 of 34 ligands with 4--6 rotatable bonds. Among the 11 ligands
with 7--8 rotatable bonds, MCF recovered eight, compared with six for
Qwen and Torsional Diffusion. The most flexible group contained only
four ligands; its apparently favorable Qwen recovery was based on three
successes. These small groups identify observed successes and failures,
but do not establish broadly applicable preferences for a chemical class.
Size and flexibility were not adjusted for one another.

\subsection{Exploratory reference coverage and sample precision}

The separate 23-molecule panel assessed whether recovery of observed
conformations coincided with frequent sampling near them. These estimates
describe supplied pools without common PoseBusters filtering, and the
evaluators differed in their treatment of failed RMSD calculations.
They therefore provide an exploratory comparison rather than a test of
the common validity criterion used for CASF.

Qwen and LoQI each covered approximately 91\% of the experimental
references on average (Table~\ref{tab:druglike}). The paired coverage
difference was unresolved (95\% confidence interval, $-9.1$ to
8.7 percentage points). LoQI had higher observed-reference precision:
Qwen's precision was lower by 12.2 percentage points
(95\% confidence interval, 4.7--19.8; Figure~\ref{fig:druglike}A).
The difference concerns these supplied pools and requires confirmation
under common filtering and failure handling. Matching-distance summaries
are provided in Table~S4.

Similar mean coverage concealed opposing molecule-level outcomes:
Qwen covered more imatinib references, whereas LoQI covered more
actinonin references (Figure~\ref{fig:druglike}B).
Experimental records need not represent distinct states or exhaust
accessible conformations, so low precision relative to these records
does not establish that unmatched conformers are physically inaccessible.
