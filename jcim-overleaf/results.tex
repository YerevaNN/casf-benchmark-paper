We compare geometric diversity and recovery of observed bound conformations across
PLINDER, CASF core, and CASF ref. PLINDER provides multiple experimental references per
molecule, while CASF provides individual references and stored ChEMBL3D ensembles for comparison.

On PLINDER, FlowR and raw torsion sampling produce the most clusters at both radii,
followed by Qwen at 0.5 \AA{} (Table~\ref{tab:diversity}). Rankings change across
datasets and clustering radii, but FlowR produces the most clusters at 1.0 \AA{}
in all three panels.

\input{\manuscriptroot tables/diversity}

FlowR also has the smallest mean largest-cluster fraction in all three panels,
whereas LoQI and the minimized CASF torsion baseline concentrate more conformers in one cluster.
The stored ChEMBL3D ensembles combine relatively few clusters with less concentration
than those two methods. Cluster counts and occupancy describe geometric variation;
neither establishes proximity to observed bound conformations.

Table~\ref{tab:count-recovery} compares MAT-R and reference coverage on PLINDER,
alongside Best RMSD and single-reference recovery on CASF.

\input{\manuscriptroot tables/count-recovery}

Qwen has the lowest PLINDER MAT-R, followed by LoQI, while LoQI has the highest
reference coverage at both cutoffs. At 0.75 \AA{}, their coverage is close,
at 91.0\% for Qwen and 91.8\% for LoQI. The two CASF
panels show a related dependence on the metric: among generated methods, LoQI leads at the
tighter cutoff, whereas Qwen has the highest recovery at the primary cutoff and the lowest
Best RMSD, although its mean distance on core is nearly identical to LoQI's. The stored
ChEMBL3D ensembles remain competitive; on ref, their primary-cutoff
recovery exceeds that of every generator at the ChEMBL-count target. A method's ranking
therefore depends on whether the objective is recovering more experimental references
within a specified tolerance or reducing the average distance to those references.

The minimized PLINDER classical pipelines produce fewer clusters and higher coverage
at the primary cutoff than their raw counterparts. At the tighter cutoff, coverage
falls for RDKit and is nearly unchanged for torsion sampling. The smaller
cluster counts therefore do not imply a uniform change in recovery of bound geometries.

Broad geometric variation alone does not explain these recovery patterns. On PLINDER,
FlowR produces many more clusters than LoQI but remains farther from the experimental
references (Figure~\ref{fig:diversity-recovery}A). The CASF comparisons show a related
distinction (Figure~\ref{fig:diversity-recovery}B,C): raw torsion sampling and FlowR produce
similar or greater cluster counts than Qwen but have higher Best RMSD, while LoQI and
ChEMBL3D approach the references with fewer clusters. Diversity is consequently an
incomplete indicator of recovery of experimental conformations.
Sample precision measures the fraction of generated conformers within the specified
RMSD cutoff of at least one experimental reference (Supporting Figure~\ref{fig:plinder-thresholds}B).

Energy variation also differs among geometrically diverse ensembles (Figure~\ref{fig:energy-dispersion}).
On PLINDER, raw torsion sampling has by far the widest typical energy spread, followed
by Torsional Diffusion and Qwen. FlowR combines the highest mean cluster count with a
narrower typical energy spread than those three methods, while LoQI and the minimized
baselines have narrower spreads still. On both CASF panels, Qwen and FlowR have narrower
typical energy distributions than raw torsion sampling and Torsional Diffusion.
Extreme ensembles make mean energy spreads much larger than medians for several
methods, including raw torsion sampling, Torsional Diffusion, and Qwen on PLINDER
(Supporting Table~\ref{tab:energy-summary}).

Increasing the CASF candidate target from the ChEMBL count to 1,000 improves recovery
and reduces Best RMSD for every evaluated generator in both core and ref
(Table~\ref{tab:recovery}). On ref, Qwen's recovery rises from 75.6\% to 91.7\%, while
Best RMSD decreases from 0.551 to 0.328 \AA{}. The stored ChEMBL3D ensemble recovers
78.3\% of references. Qwen retains the highest recovery among generators in both panels
at the larger target, reaching 92.6\% on core, where the training filter removed both
ligand matches and their specified similarity neighborhoods. FlowR produces the most
clusters. Additional sampling therefore expands geometric variation and experimental recovery to different degrees across methods.

Aggregate recovery can conceal differences in molecular difficulty. In CASF core and ref,
recovery generally declines for larger or more flexible ligands, although the methods differ
in how strongly performance falls (Figure~\ref{fig:size}). CASF ref extends the comparison
to more populated strata; the sparse core groups cannot support stable method rankings
(Supporting Figure~\ref{fig:supporting-composition}). These patterns caution against interpreting a panel-wide average
as uniform coverage of the chemistry needed for a conformational resource.

Similar reference coverage can accompany different sample precision. On PLINDER,
LoQI and Qwen recover similar fractions of references at 0.75 \AA{}, but LoQI has
substantially higher precision: 60.5\% versus 44.8\% (Table~\ref{tab:druglike}).
Supporting Figure~\ref{fig:supporting-plinder-coverage} shows how both measures vary across individual molecules.
The minimized torsion pipeline has higher precision than raw torsion sampling,
55.0\% versus 37.7\%, while producing fewer clusters. Its samples are thus more
concentrated near observed conformations despite representing fewer shapes.

The coverage--precision tradeoff also depends on matching tolerance
(Supporting Figure~\ref{fig:plinder-thresholds}). LoQI has higher
coverage at 0.5 \AA{}, while Qwen 1.7B FSQ has higher coverage at 1.0 \AA{};
LoQI maintains higher sample precision than Qwen 1.7B FSQ at both and at the primary cutoff.

The conformational maps relate generated ensembles to reference collections with
contrasting geometric breadth (Figure~\ref{fig:conformer-landscapes}). Staurosporine has
117 references in a mean of 3.1 geometric clusters at 0.5 \AA{}, whereas actinonin has
82 references in 34.9 clusters. Reference counts alone therefore do not describe the
variation among the observed bound geometries.

For staurosporine, all three displayed methods recover every reference, but sample
precision ranges from 28.5\% to 93.8\%. For actinonin, LoQI and Qwen recover 67.1\%
and 42.7\% of references, respectively, whereas FlowR recovers none at the same cutoff;
sample precision is low for all three. Distance-based shading locates generated conformers
relative to the observations without treating projected overlap as a match. These cases
illustrate different relationships between reference recovery and sample concentration,
rather than their prevalence across the panel. Unmatched conformers are not thereby
classified as physically inaccessible.

\conformerlandscapefigure

The molecule-level distances show that different molecules account for the largest
errors across methods (Supporting Figure~\ref{fig:plinder-molecule-tracking}). Imatinib has the
largest MAT-R for LoQI, whereas Amprenavir has the largest for Qwen 1.7B FSQ.
These molecule-level differences qualify the aggregate rankings: Qwen has the lowest
mean MAT-R on PLINDER, but other methods provide closer matches for particular molecules.

Protein-contact pharmacophore queries directly test whether a library
contains the complete arrangement of ligand features observed in a binding site
(Supporting Figure~\ref{fig:pharmacophore-query}A,B). At the 0.5 \AA{} tolerance and
a requested budget of 1,000, the equal Qwen/LoQI library covers 78.1\% of eligible
queries, compared with 71.2\% for Qwen and 73.6\% for LoQI. Its paired improvement
over Qwen is 6.9 percentage points (95\% molecule-bootstrap interval, 0.3 to 15.4
points). The six learned single-method libraries span 67.1--73.6\%, while raw and
minimized RDKit cover 52.1\% and 52.9\%, respectively. Keeping PoseBusters failures
in the sampling denominator changes the mixture endpoint only from 78.1\% to 77.6\%.
Pharmacophore and whole-molecule recovery are not interchangeable: for Qwen, 6.9\%
of candidate--query comparisons satisfy only the pharmacophore criterion and 7.8\%
satisfy only the 0.75 \AA{} whole-molecule RMSD criterion after equal weighting by
molecule.

The generator-blind extension broadens the analysis to 311 eligible queries from
44 molecules (Supporting Figure~\ref{fig:pharmacophore-query}C,D). The mixture,
Qwen, and LoQI cover 71.7\%, 70.3\%, and 68.3\%, respectively, while minimized and
raw RDKit cover 57.3\% and 46.7\%. The mixture--Qwen difference is only 1.4
percentage points (95\% molecule-bootstrap interval, $-1.8$ to 5.5 points), so the
larger panel does not confirm the pilot's mixture advantage. PoseBusters gating
changes the mixture endpoint by less than 0.01 percentage point. Qwen coverage is
66.4\% among the 17 eligible identities with an exact isomeric InChIKey match in
its audited training export and 72.8\% among the other 27; these descriptive strata
have wide, overlapping intervals and do not exclude scaffold or near-neighbor
overlap.

As a final allocation experiment, we expanded the PLINDER-22 analysis from one
protein-contact query definition to four CDPKit query families: features from an
individual ligand pose, interaction features from an individual complex, ligand
features shared across poses, and interaction features shared in an aligned protein
site (Supporting Figure~\ref{fig:cdpkit-query-families}). This design produced
57,459 queries from 809 parent hypotheses. We compared pure Qwen and LoQI with
fixed 75:25, 50:50, and 25:75 allocations at four attempt budgets. The main
comparison was specified as four of four features within 0.5~\AA{}; the complete
primary grid contains 2,352 family--requirement--radius--allocation--budget cells,
rather than only cells favoring a particular allocation.

At the smallest evaluated budget of 20, the 50:50 allocation has the highest
mean coverage in all four families, spanning 80.26--87.54\%
(Table~\ref{tab:pharmacophore-mixture}; Figure~\ref{fig:pharmacophore-mixture}A--D).
Its gain over pure Qwen is 2.43--3.19 percentage points. The paired intervals
exclude zero for both individual-query families but include zero for both shared
families (Figure~\ref{fig:pharmacophore-mixture}E). The corresponding gain over
pure LoQI is 5.52--6.57 points, with all four intervals excluding zero. In contrast,
50:50 is only 0.02--0.19 point above 75:25, and all four paired intervals include
zero. Thus, the equal and Qwen-heavy mixtures are not distinguished at this
budget despite the consistent numerical ordering.

At budget 1,000, the 50:50 allocation covers 98.39--99.59\% of the reference
queries and remains within 0.28 percentage point of the best family-specific
allocation, although Qwen or 75:25 has the highest mean in every family
(Supporting Table~\ref{tab:cdpkit-endpoint-coverage}). Its mean effective cluster
count is 131.8, compared with 66.7 for LoQI, 185.1 for 75:25, and 233.2 for Qwen
(Figure~\ref{fig:pharmacophore-mixture}F). The corresponding valid yields are
90.5, 89.9, 90.9, and 91.2\%, respectively. The full primary grid shows the same
broad distinction: Qwen reaches more hypotheses, whereas LoQI places a larger
fraction of its valid conformers inside hypotheses that it reaches (Supporting
Figure~\ref{fig:cdpkit-generator-separation}).
