\subsection{Experimental-conformation recovery depended on the required agreement}

We first ranked generators by recovery of the experimental ligand geometry
in the 94-entry core cohort. Generated ensembles used a target of 1,000
candidates before PoseBusters filtering; ChEMBL3D-PB was evaluated as its
finite stored ensemble. Qwen denotes the 1.7B FSQ pretraining checkpoint at
step 47,023 throughout the main comparison. The principal recovery cutoff
was 0.75~\AA{}, with all mapped entries included in the denominator.
% AUTHOR: report how the representative checkpoint was selected. It was not
% the highest-scoring Qwen on every endpoint; preserve the full inventory in SI.

Qwen recovered 86 of 94 targets (91.5\%), compared with 84 for Torsional
Diffusion (89.4\%), 81 for LoQI (86.2\%), 76 for RDKit without minimization
(80.9\%), and 74 for ChEMBL3D-PB (78.7\%; Table~\ref{tab:recovery}).
Relative to ChEMBL3D-PB, Qwen retained all recovered targets and added 12,
a paired difference of 12.8 percentage points (95\% confidence interval,
6.4--20.2). This comparison concerns the available ensembles: Qwen retained
972.6 conformers per entry on average, whereas ChEMBL3D-PB contained 81.2.

\input{tables/recovery}

The ordering changed when closer agreement was required. At 0.5~\AA{},
LoQI recovered 83.0\% of core targets, compared with 78.7\% for Qwen;
at 0.25~\AA{}, the corresponding values were 55.3\% and 53.2\%.
Thus, Qwen's lead at the principal cutoff did not extend to every structural
tolerance. The recovery curves in Figure~\ref{fig:recovery}A place the
principal comparison within this threshold dependence.

The larger ref cohort supported the high-budget recovery result at
0.75~\AA{}: Qwen recovered 92.5\% of 1,236 entries, compared with 86.8\%
for LoQI and 78.7\% for ChEMBL3D-PB. The paired Qwen advantage over LoQI
was 5.7 percentage points (95\% confidence interval, 3.8--7.7). Excluding
compounds shared with core preserved the Qwen advantage over ChEMBL3D-PB
(14.0 points; 11.7--16.4). This sensitivity addresses overlap between the
evaluation cohorts, rather than overlap with training data.

Better recovery described the best members of the ensemble. On core,
Qwen's mean minimum RMSD was 0.307~\AA{}, compared with 0.339~\AA{} for
LoQI and 0.525~\AA{} for ChEMBL3D-PB. Their mean within-ensemble median
RMSDs were much closer: 1.318, 1.305, and 1.326~\AA{}, respectively.
The recovery advantage therefore did not imply a corresponding improvement
in the typical generated conformer.

\subsection{Smaller sampling targets changed the comparison}

Figure~\ref{fig:recovery}B compares sampling targets. We reduced generated
candidate pools to the
stereoisomer-specific ChEMBL3D count before applying the validity filter.
On core, Qwen recovery fell from 91.5\% at the fixed target to 80.9\% at
this smaller target, with a mean of 78.3 retained conformers. ChEMBL3D-PB
recovered 78.7\% with 81.2 conformers. The paired difference was only
two entries (2.1 percentage points; 95\% confidence interval, $-4.3$ to 8.5).

On ref, the smaller target reversed the ordering: Qwen recovered 76.3\%
with a mean of 63.3 retained conformers, compared with 78.7\% and 66.2
for ChEMBL3D-PB. The paired difference was $-2.4$ percentage points
(95\% confidence interval, $-4.7$ to $-0.1$). These endpoints show that
high-budget recovery does not establish superior recovery from a compact
ensemble. Because retained counts differed, they do not identify a crossover
at a particular valid-conformer count.

\subsection{Geometric diversity did not provide the same ranking as recovery}

Cluster counts separated broad geometric sampling from recovery of the
experimental target. At a 1.0~\AA{} clustering radius, FlowR produced a
mean of 114.4 clusters per core entry, compared with 72.8 for Qwen, but
recovered fewer targets (86.2\% versus 91.5\%). Torsion perturbation
produced a similar number of clusters to Qwen (74.2) but recovered 78.7\%.
LoQI recovered 86.2\% with only 20.2 clusters. The same contrasts were
present on ref: FlowR, Qwen, and LoQI produced 105.4, 63.7, and 24.4
clusters, respectively, with recovery of 85.5\%, 92.5\%, and 86.8\%.
These comparisons show that cluster count alone did not reproduce the
recovery ranking under the evaluated sampling conditions
(Figure~\ref{fig:diversity-recovery}B).

Figure~\ref{fig:diversity-recovery}A compares cluster counts across radii,
so that the diversity comparison can be examined at several structural
resolutions. Cluster counts remain dependent on retained sample number and
clustering order; they do not measure the number of biologically accessible
states.

\subsection{Molecular size and flexibility identified harder recovery problems}

We next stratified recovery by heavy-atom count
(Table~\ref{tab:size}; Figure~\ref{fig:size}). On core, Qwen, LoQI, and
FlowR each recovered all 39 entries with fewer than 20 heavy atoms. Their
performance separated among larger ligands. For the 12 entries with
30--39 heavy atoms, recovery was 66.7\% for Qwen, 58.3\% for LoQI,
41.7\% for ChEMBL3D-PB, and 33.3\% for FlowR. Core contained only two
entries with at least 40 heavy atoms, so that group could not support a
stable ranking.

\input{tables/size}

The larger cohort provided more observations in these groups. Among its
152 entries with 30--39 heavy atoms, Qwen recovered 71.7\%, LoQI 64.5\%,
ChEMBL3D-PB 50.0\%, and FlowR 41.4\%. Recovery remained low among the
24 entries with at least 40 heavy atoms: Qwen and LoQI recovered eight and
seven, respectively. The results support a size-dependent comparison, while
leaving substantial difficulty at the largest sizes.

Flexibility stratification gave a related pattern. On ref, Qwen recovery
was 99.4\%, 94.1\%, 73.1\%, and 33.3\% for ligands with 0--3, 4--6,
7--8, and at least 9 rotatable bonds, respectively. ChEMBL3D-PB recovered
94.2\%, 73.6\%, 37.0\%, and 17.6\% in the same groups. The greatest
absolute gain occurred in the 7--8-rotor group, rather than the most flexible
group. Size and flexibility are related descriptors, and these stratified
comparisons do not isolate either as a cause of method performance.

\subsection{Multiple experimental references separated coverage from concentration}

The separate 23-molecule drug panel extended recovery to 2,450 experimental
reference records. Coverage was calculated on the supplied generated pools,
with validity assessed separately. These results are descriptive because
common PoseBusters filtering and RMSD-failure handling have not yet been
harmonized across methods (Methods).

Qwen and LoQI had similar mean reference coverage (COV-R), 91.0\% and
91.2\%, respectively (Table~\ref{tab:druglike}). Their paired difference
was $-0.25$ percentage points (95\% confidence interval, $-9.1$ to 8.7),
which did not resolve a recall winner or establish equivalence. LoQI had
higher observed-reference precision (COV-P): 59.0\% versus 46.8\% for
Qwen, a paired Qwen-minus-LoQI difference of $-12.2$ points ($-19.8$ to
$-4.7$). Thus, similar coverage of the observations accompanied different
concentrations of generated samples near those observations
(Figure~\ref{fig:druglike}).

\input{tables/druglike}

Molecule-level results showed complementary successes and failures. Qwen
covered 78.9\% of the 57 imatinib references, compared with 5.3\% for
LoQI; for the 99 actinonin references, the corresponding values were 4.0\%
and 67.7\%. These contrasts show why an average recovery score does not
describe performance on every molecule. Low observed-reference precision
does not, by itself, establish that unmatched conformers are inaccessible;
the experimental collection is incomplete and its records need not
represent distinct conformational states.
