We compare geometric diversity and recovery of observed bound conformations across
PLINDER, CASF core, and CASF ref. PLINDER provides multiple experimental references per
molecule, while CASF provides individual references and stored ChEMBL3D ensembles for comparison.

Across the three sets, FlowR, Qwen, and raw torsion sampling generally produce more
clusters than LoQI and the minimized CASF baselines (Table~\ref{tab:diversity}). Their
ordering changes with the dataset and clustering radius, but FlowR produces the most
clusters at 1.0 \AA{} in all three panels.

\input{\manuscriptroot tables/diversity}

FlowR also has the smallest mean largest-cluster fraction in all three panels,
whereas LoQI and the minimized CASF torsion baseline concentrate more conformers in one cluster.
The stored ChEMBL3D ensembles combine relatively few clusters with less concentration
than those two methods. Cluster counts and occupancy describe geometric variation;
neither establishes proximity to observed bound conformations.

Table~\ref{tab:count-recovery} compares proximity to the experimental conformations through
reference coverage and matching distance.

\input{\manuscriptroot tables/count-recovery}

LoQI has the highest PLINDER coverage at both cutoffs, while Qwen has the lowest mean matching
distance. At 0.75 \AA{}, their coverage is close, at 91.8\% and 91.0\%, respectively. The two CASF
panels show a related dependence on the metric: among generated methods, LoQI leads at the
tighter cutoff, whereas Qwen has the highest recovery at the primary cutoff and the lowest
Best RMSD, although its mean distance on core is nearly identical to LoQI's. The stored
ChEMBL3D ensembles remain competitive; on ref, their primary-cutoff
recovery exceeds that of every generator at the ChEMBL-count target. A method's ranking
therefore depends on whether the objective is recovering more experimental references
within a specified tolerance or reducing the average distance to those references.

Minimizing the existing PLINDER classical pools reduces geometric diversity and increases
coverage at the primary cutoff, but coverage falls at the tighter cutoff. The smaller
cluster counts therefore do not imply a uniform change in recovery of bound geometries.

Broad geometric variation alone does not explain these recovery patterns. On PLINDER,
FlowR produces many more clusters but covers fewer references than LoQI
(Figure~\ref{fig:druglike}A--B).

\drugfigure

The CASF comparisons show a related distinction (Figure~\ref{fig:diversity-recovery}).
Raw torsion sampling and FlowR produce similar or greater cluster counts than Qwen but have
higher Best RMSD, while LoQI and ChEMBL3D approach the references with fewer clusters. Diversity is consequently
an incomplete indicator of recovery of experimental conformations.

\diversityfigure

Energy variation also differs among geometrically diverse ensembles. On both CASF panels,
Qwen and FlowR have narrower typical energy distributions than raw torsion sampling and
Torsional Diffusion, while LoQI, the minimized baselines, and ChEMBL3D have narrower
distributions still (Figure~\ref{fig:energy-dispersion}). Extreme ensembles make mean
energy spreads much larger than medians for several methods, particularly Torsional
Diffusion and raw torsion sampling on ref (Supporting Table S6).

\energyfigure

Increasing the CASF candidate target from the ChEMBL count to 1,000 improves recovery
and reduces Best RMSD for every evaluated generator in both core and ref
(Table~\ref{tab:recovery}). On ref, Qwen's recovery rises from 75.6\% to 91.7\%, while
Best RMSD decreases from 0.551 to 0.328 \AA{}. The stored ChEMBL3D ensemble recovers
78.3\% of references. Qwen retains the highest recovery among generators in both panels
at the larger target, reaching 92.6\% on core, where the training filter removed both
ligand matches and their specified similarity neighborhoods. FlowR produces the most
clusters. Additional sampling therefore expands geometric variation and experimental recovery to different degrees across methods.

Several generators exceed the recovery of the stored ChEMBL3D ensembles at the larger
target in both CASF panels (Figure~\ref{fig:recovery}). This gain does not imply
retaining every stored-ensemble hit: on ref, Qwen recovers 155 additional ligands but
misses 15 recovered by ChEMBL3D (Supporting Figure S3).

Aggregate recovery can conceal differences in molecular difficulty. In CASF core and ref,
recovery generally declines for larger or more flexible ligands, although the methods differ
in how strongly performance falls (Figure~\ref{fig:size}). CASF ref extends the comparison
to more populated strata; the sparse core groups cannot support stable method rankings
(Supporting Figure S4). These patterns caution against interpreting a panel-wide average
as uniform coverage of the chemistry needed for a conformational resource.

\sizefigure

Similar reference coverage can accompany different sample precision. On PLINDER,
LoQI and Qwen recover similar fractions of references at 0.75 \AA{}, but LoQI has
substantially higher precision: 60.5\% versus 44.8\% (Table~\ref{tab:druglike}).
Supporting Figure S5 shows how both measures vary across individual molecules.
Minimizing the independent torsion pools increases precision from 36.2\% to 56.7\%,
while reducing geometric diversity. The paired comparison shows that sampling can
become more concentrated near observed conformations even as fewer shapes are represented.

\input{\manuscriptroot tables/druglike}

The threshold curves extend this comparison (Figure~\ref{fig:druglike}). LoQI has higher
coverage at 0.5 \AA{}, while Qwen 1.7B FSQ has higher coverage at 1.0 \AA{};
LoQI maintains higher sample precision than Qwen 1.7B FSQ at both and at the primary cutoff.

The additional Qwen 0.6B CASF-dedup model ranks third in reference coverage at the
primary cutoff, but has the lowest sample precision and PoseBusters pass rate in
Table~\ref{tab:druglike}. High reference coverage therefore coexists with lower
sample concentration and validity.

The molecule-level distances show that different molecules account for the largest
errors across methods (Figure~\ref{fig:plinder-molecule-tracking}). Imatinib has the
largest MAT-R for LoQI, whereas Amprenavir has the largest for Qwen 1.7B FSQ.
For the 0.6B CASF-dedup model, Actinonin has the largest MAT-R and Amprenavir the
largest MAT-P. The same molecule need not dominate both metrics or model variants.

\moleculetrackingfigure

The Qwen comparison examines how model size and coordinate representation relate to
reference matching and structural validity. FSQ has lower mean matching distances than
binned coordinates at both Qwen model
sizes on CASF ref and PLINDER
(Figure~\ref{fig:qwen-size-representation}). Increasing size from 0.6B to 1.7B produces
smaller changes and does not consistently improve PLINDER MAT-R. On CASF ref,
FSQ improves matching for approximately 66\% of ligands at each size, compared
with 50--54\% when increasing size within a representation (Supporting Figure S2).
Actinonin contributes substantially to FSQ's mean matching advantage on PLINDER.
Scaling has a clearer effect on validity: the pooled PLINDER PoseBusters pass rate
rises from 82.7\% to 92.5\% for FSQ (Supporting Figure S6). Training exports and token exposure differ
between representations, so the matching advantage cannot be attributed to encoding alone.
