\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Selecting a generator for a conformational library requires balancing coverage of observed
bound geometries with the distribution of the generated samples. The present comparisons
show that geometric breadth, experimental recovery, and sample concentration favor different
methods, and that their ordering depends on the sampling budget and matching tolerance.
Coverage and precision are particularly useful together: an ensemble can represent many
experimental references while placing only a small fraction of its samples near them.
G{\"u}nther et al.\cite{gunther2006representation} established the value of finite conformer
libraries for representing the multiple bound shapes of a drug. Our results extend this
question to modern generation procedures by examining both the references reached and the
conformations supplied around them.

For constructing a large library, Qwen's recovery at the larger CASF budget, low matching
distances, and broad geometric sampling provide a basis for selecting it for further dataset
construction. LoQI's higher PLINDER precision favors it when concentration near the available
observations is the main objective. This tradeoff should be assessed at the intended ensemble
size: the budget-dependent ordering shows that performance with a small pool does not fully
predict performance with a larger one. Additional sampling also complements the stored
ChEMBL3D resource without reproducing every conformation that it already covers. Retaining
useful stored conformers alongside new generations is therefore worth evaluating, although
we have not assessed a combined library or its storage and computational costs.

Postprocessing is part of this choice because it changes the conformational distribution.
The different responses to minimization at the two matching tolerances show that its value
depends on the required geometric agreement. McNutt et al.\cite{mcnutt2023conformers} found
benefits from minimization while also showing that selecting only the lowest-energy
conformers was insufficient for the best retrieval of bound geometries. Our paired comparisons
similarly argue for evaluating postprocessing against the intended structural criterion.
The energy analysis provides a complementary description: broad geometric sampling can
coexist with a narrower typical energy spread. A smaller spread alone, however, does not
establish lower strain or physical plausibility, and the single-point force-field energies
do not identify occupied energy basins or measure binding free energies.

The Qwen comparisons suggest that matching experimental geometry and improving structural
validity can respond differently to model configuration. The more consistent matching
advantage of the tested FSQ configurations motivates further evaluation of the coordinate
representation, while the clearer validity benefit from increasing model size supports
assessing these outcomes separately when scaling models. Training exports and token exposure
differ across representations, so the comparison does not isolate an encoding effect.
The concentration of some PLINDER differences in particular molecules also limits how broadly
an average improvement can be interpreted. These findings concern the evaluated checkpoints;
replicated training runs would be needed to establish their stability.

The exclusion policy gives the panels different scopes. For Qwen, core and PLINDER
assess recovery after removing benchmark matches and their specified fingerprint
neighborhoods from the training corpus. Ref excludes normalized identity matches
without systematically excluding related molecules. Qwen's leading CASF recovery is
observed under both policies, including the broader core exclusions. However, the
similar recovery rates on core and ref do not measure the effect of neighborhood
removal: the panels differ in composition, size, and experimental references. Such an
effect would require comparing models trained with different exclusion policies on
the same evaluation molecules. These Qwen-specific exclusions also do not establish
matched training separation for the external pretrained generators.

Several features of the evaluation limit generalization. CASF is restricted to the intersection
with ChEMBL3D, and PLINDER contains a small, manually selected panel with multiple observed
structures. Large and flexible molecules remain sparsely represented in parts of CASF.
Experimental references cover only part of accessible conformational space, so low precision
against them does not establish that unmatched conformers are irrelevant to binding.
CASF matching uses PoseBusters-filtered ensembles, whereas PLINDER matching uses the supplied
pools; their absolute performance levels are therefore not directly comparable. Candidate
budgets also do not equalize computational cost. The v2 Qwen training records document
benchmark exclusions, but a final audit of residual overlap and the older model's training history remains necessary before making a general
claim of evaluation on unseen molecules.

The benchmark provides a basis for choosing a procedure to construct a reusable conformational
resource for structure-based drug design and learning molecular geometry, including
pharmacophore-conditioned generation. The released dataset will need its own assessment of
molecular coverage, validity, diversity, and energy distributions. Its value for docking,
screening, or model training must also be tested directly. McNutt et al.\cite{mcnutt2023conformers}
showed that larger ensembles can improve structural recovery without improving screening
performance. The structural advantages measured here therefore motivate downstream evaluation
but do not establish its outcome.
