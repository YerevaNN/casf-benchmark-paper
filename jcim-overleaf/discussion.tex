\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Selecting a generator for a conformational library requires balancing coverage of observed
bound geometries with the distribution of the generated samples. The present comparisons
show that geometric breadth, experimental recovery, and sample concentration favor different
methods, and that their ordering depends on the sampling budget and matching tolerance.
Coverage and precision are particularly useful together: an ensemble can represent many
experimental references while placing only a small fraction of its samples near them.
G{\"u}nther et al.\cite{gunther2006representation} established the value of finite conformer
libraries for representing the multiple bound shapes of a drug. Our results extend this
question to modern generation procedures by examining both the references reached and the
conformations supplied around them.

For constructing a large library, Qwen's recovery at the larger CASF budget, low matching
distances, and broad geometric sampling provide a basis for selecting it for further dataset
construction. LoQI's higher PLINDER precision favors it when concentration near the available
observations is the main objective. This tradeoff should be assessed at the intended ensemble
size: the budget-dependent ordering shows that performance with a small pool does not fully
predict performance with a larger one. Additional sampling also complements the stored
ChEMBL3D resource without reproducing every conformation that it already covers. Retaining
useful stored conformers alongside new generations is therefore worth evaluating, although
we have not assessed a combined library or its storage and computational costs.

Postprocessing is part of this choice because it changes the conformational distribution.
The different responses to minimization at the two matching tolerances show that its value
depends on the required geometric agreement. McNutt et al.\cite{mcnutt2023conformers} found
benefits from minimization while also showing that selecting only the lowest-energy
conformers was insufficient for the best retrieval of bound geometries. Our paired PLINDER RDKit
comparison similarly supports evaluating postprocessing against the intended structural
criterion. The torsion comparison concerns separately sampled pipelines and does not
isolate the effect of minimization on identical conformers.
The energy analysis provides a complementary description: broad geometric sampling can
coexist with a narrower typical energy spread. Qwen's reference-matching performance
on PLINDER does not imply the narrowest energy distribution, supporting separate
evaluation of these properties. A smaller spread alone, however, does not
establish lower strain or physical plausibility, and the single-point force-field energies
do not identify occupied energy basins or measure binding free energies.

The Qwen comparisons (Supporting Figure~\ref{fig:qwen-size-representation}) suggest
that matching experimental geometry and improving structural
validity can respond differently to model configuration. The more consistent matching
advantage of the tested FSQ configurations motivates further evaluation of the coordinate
representation, while the clearer validity benefit from increasing model size supports
assessing these outcomes separately when scaling models. Training exports and token exposure
differ across representations, so the comparison does not isolate an encoding effect.
The concentration of some PLINDER differences in particular molecules also limits how broadly
an average improvement can be interpreted. These findings concern the evaluated checkpoints;
replicated training runs would be needed to establish their stability.

The four Qwen configurations share a training exclusion policy: normalized molecular
matches and specified similarity neighborhoods are removed around core and PLINDER
ligands, while ref adds normalized identity exclusions only. Ref analogues can still
be removed through the core or PLINDER exclusions. Under this shared policy, the
primary Qwen model has the highest recovery among generators at 0.75 \AA{} on both
CASF panels. Similar recovery rates across these panels do not measure the effect of
neighborhood removal, because the evaluation molecules differ. Isolating that effect
would require varying the training exclusions while holding the evaluation panel fixed.
The external generators have not been shown to use equivalent training exclusions.

The protein-contact pharmacophore analysis asks a related but distinct structural
question. The Qwen/LoQI gain in the 22-molecule panel indicates that two libraries
can complement one another at the level of complete bound feature arrangements,
even when neither library uniformly dominates by whole-molecule distance. The
candidate-level disagreements between pharmacophore and RMSD criteria are expected:
a conformer can reproduce the required feature centers without recovering every
heavy-atom position, or achieve a low global RMSD while missing a tight feature
assignment. In the generator-blind extension, Qwen and LoQI retain advantages over
both RDKit baselines, but the mixture's small increment over Qwen has a paired
interval spanning zero; complementarity at the pilot magnitude is therefore not
confirmed over the larger set. Exact Qwen training identity is not associated with
higher coverage in the descriptive stratification, but the small strata, wide
intervals, and possibility of analogue overlap preclude either a causal training-data
interpretation or an unseen-molecule generalization claim. These coverage results
also do not establish virtual-screening enrichment or docking performance.

The final four-family experiment turns this complementarity question into a fixed
allocation decision. It does not identify one allocation that dominates every metric
and budget: Qwen and 75:25 retain more geometric breadth, and one of them has the
highest high-budget mean in each query family. At budget 20, however, 50:50 is the
numerical leader under all four independently constructed query families and is
numerically above either pure source in the aggregate means. It also preserves nearly
all high-budget coverage while providing approximately twice LoQI's effective
cluster count. The evidence therefore supports 50:50 as a balanced production
default for small per-molecule libraries, rather than as a universal optimum.

This recommendation is deliberately operational. The 50:50 and 75:25 means are
effectively tied at budget 20, so the data do not estimate 50:50 as uniquely superior.
Equal allocation is preferable when no molecule-specific oracle is available because
it gives both generators equal opportunity to contribute, avoids fitting a mixture
weight to only 22 molecules, and has the strongest observed small-budget profile
without a material high-budget coverage loss. If geometric breadth is valued above
source balance, the results instead support Qwen or 75:25. The complete primary grid
and paired intervals are retained to make this policy distinction visible; no
post hoc combined coverage--diversity score is used.

Several features of the evaluation limit generalization. CASF is restricted to the intersection
with ChEMBL3D, and PLINDER contains a small, manually selected panel with multiple observed
structures. Large and flexible molecules remain sparsely represented in parts of CASF.
Experimental references cover only part of accessible conformational space, so low precision
against them does not establish that unmatched conformers are irrelevant to binding.
CASF matching uses PoseBusters-filtered ensembles, whereas PLINDER matching uses the supplied
pools; their absolute performance levels are therefore not directly comparable. Candidate
budgets also do not equalize computational cost. Training exclusions are documented
for the four Qwen size/representation configurations, but residual overlap, including
tokenizer development data, requires verification. These records do not
establish evaluation on unseen molecules for every model.

The benchmark provides a basis for choosing a procedure to construct a reusable conformational
resource for structure-based drug design and learning molecular geometry, including
pharmacophore-conditioned generation. The released dataset will need its own assessment of
molecular coverage, validity, diversity, and energy distributions. Its value for docking,
screening, or model training must also be tested directly. McNutt et al.\cite{mcnutt2023conformers}
showed that larger ensembles can improve structural recovery without improving screening
performance. The structural advantages measured here therefore motivate downstream evaluation
but do not establish its outcome.
