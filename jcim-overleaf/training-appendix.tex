\section*{CoordToken and Qwen training records}

\subsection*{Geometry representation and tokenizer training}

CoordToken\cite{geikyan2026coordtoken} encodes canonical isomeric SMILES together
with centered heavy-atom coordinates. Coordinates follow the SMILES atom order;
non-atom tokens retain their graph information but carry no coordinate target.
The encoder and decoder each have eight transformer layers, width 1,024, 16 attention
heads, and feed-forward width 4,096. A 12-dimensional bottleneck quantizes each scalar
to two levels, giving 4,096 possible codes without a learned vector codebook.
One code is transmitted for each atom, together with one learned molecular register
code. The decoder receives these codes and SMILES identities and predicts Cartesian
coordinates. Training minimizes coordinate mean squared error over atom positions,
masking non-atom tokens and padding.

The benchmark uses the separate filtered release dated 20 October 2026, whose
checkpoint SHA-256 begins \texttt{37e5aa88c6e3de1d}. It is not the full-corpus model
used for the headline reconstruction results in the CoordToken paper. Both tokenizer
epochs use the same 187,398,101-record training manifest. Training begins from random
initialization and uses eight H100 GPUs, global batch 512, AdamW with peak learning
rate $5\times10^{-5}$, $\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.999$, dropout 0.05, and gradient
clipping at norm 0.1. Weight decay is 0.01 for matrix parameters and zero for biases
and normalization parameters. The first epoch warms up over 1,024,000 examples,
maintains the peak rate until 90\% of the epoch, and then decays to zero by a cosine
schedule. The second epoch resumes the model and optimizer state, restores the peak
rate, and decays over its final 20\%. Each epoch contains 366,012 updates. The initial
seed is 123 and the second-epoch shuffle seed is 124. Computation uses bfloat16 with
FP32 coordinate and quantization operations; the recorded environment is PyTorch
2.6.0 with CUDA 12.4. Monitoring uses a fixed 4,952-record development cohort.
Full checkpoint and data hashes are archived with the training records.

\subsection*{Corpus splitting and exact deduplication}

The source release was assembled before the CASF/PLINDER exclusions. A common parser
and RDKit 2025.09.6 validated molecular strings, finite coordinate triplets, and
agreement between atom and coordinate counts. Molecular identity for split assignment
was the full Standard InChIKey of the sanitized decoded SMILES, retaining encoded
stereochemistry and isotopes. No additional salt stripping, stereochemistry removal,
or scaffold matching was applied. Unresolved identity failures were quarantined,
rather than treated as new molecules; nine records remained in quarantine. The
prepared inputs include source-checked ChEMBL3D replacements and historically filtered
collections, so their counts do not reconstruct every original source release.

For sources with predefined splits, test identities took precedence over validation,
and validation over training. Lower-priority rows matching protected identities were
excluded. KIBA-3D, OMol25-small, PubChem3D, and ZINC were then assigned splits in that
order, targeting 0.5\% of each input pool's rows for each of validation and test.
Selection used whole InChIKey groups ranked deterministically with seed 42, so quotas
could be exceeded. Previously assigned holdout identities were respected; remaining
quotas were filled only with unassigned identities. Holdout candidates omitted because
of a quota were not returned to training. For nablaDFT, validation targeted 0.5\% of its full collection, taking existing
validation matches first and filling the remainder from identities absent from the
other sources' retained training sets. Its predefined conformation test deliberately
retained molecular overlap with training, while its structure and scaffold tests
were protected by exact InChIKey matching. This is molecular separation, not scaffold
separation. GEOM-Revisited was also protected and retained as a separate test file.

After merging, exact equality meant identical coordinate-enriched strings, with no
rounding, coordinate alignment, or comparison of record names. Hash candidates were
confirmed by full-string comparison. Within each merged split, the first occurrence
was retained and removed records were linked to it through a provenance mapping.
This removed 1,734,217 training, 7,102 validation, and 7,557 test records, leaving
189,792,028, 1,802,906, and 5,553,421, respectively. The final audit found no repeated
strings within these merged files or exact strings shared across splits. The separate
GEOM-Revisited file contains 23,404 records, of which 2,037 also occur in GEOM test;
this overlap is confined to test resources. Exact-string deduplication does not remove
all geometrically equivalent conformers or near-duplicates. Corpus split audits and
CASF/PLINDER exclusions address different forms of overlap.

\subsection*{Training collection and benchmark exclusions}

The source collection contains 189,792,028 training records. After benchmark
exclusions, its largest components are PubChem3D (92,818,460 records), ZINC
(73,082,070), nablaDFT (8,623,394), GEOM (5,016,456), and ChEMBL3D (1,398,651).
Other records come from the OMol25 biological- and small-molecule collections,
SAIR, BindingNet, Binding MOAD, CrossDocked2020, HiQBind, DAVIS-3D, KIBA-3D,
Kinodata-3D, PLINDER, and SPINDR. Counts refer to conformer records, not distinct
molecular identities. The complete source counts before and after filtering are
provided in the companion \texttt{filter-per-source.csv}.

For exclusion matching, temporary molecular copies are uncharged using RDKit's
protonation-only mode and converted to canonical tautomers. Two keys are compared:
the connectivity block of the normalized InChIKey and a stereochemistry-independent
\texttt{HetAtomProtomer} hash. A match under either key is sufficient. The original
coordinates, charges, and stereochemistry of retained records are not rewritten.
For the 285 CASF core and original 23 PLINDER queries, the ligands and their matching
molecules found in training and validation form an expanded query set. Molecules with
ECFP4 Tanimoto similarity strictly greater than 0.4 to this set are identified using 2,048-bit radius-2 Morgan fingerprints
without chirality, calculated on the original molecular forms. Their normalized
identity matches are also excluded, along with the original full InChIKeys of the
similarity hits, so that normalization cannot return a flagged molecule to training.
Previously identified exclusions from the plain-identity and tautomer-based screens
are retained. This procedure extends
beyond exact stereoisomer matching and does not apply a recursive similarity expansion.
For the 1,236 ref ligands, only the normalized identity exclusion is added: there is
no separate search for similar molecules around ref. Some ref analogues can nevertheless
be excluded through their relationship to a core or PLINDER ligand. The same filtered
training population serves all three evaluation panels.

The executed report assigns 2,201,663 removed records to the core-related category,
163,118 additional records to PLINDER, and 29,146 additional records to ref. These
categories are mutually exclusive in that order, leaving 187,398,101 records.
For core, 197,631 records matched benchmark ligands directly under the normalized
rule, 1,944,085 were removed at the similarity stage, 59,940 matched those similar
molecules, and seven were retained as exclusions from earlier screens. Expanding the
queries to matching molecular variants identified 539,530 similarity-stage training
records beyond those found from the original ligands alone. Thus, most core-related
removals extend beyond direct matches. These stages sum to the core-related
removal category, before the separate ref exclusion.

The overall removal fraction masks different effects on the source composition:
25.99\% of Binding MOAD and 22.25\% of PLINDER records were excluded, compared with
0.97\% of PubChem3D and 0.25\% of ZINC. Retained rows were shuffled with seed
20261012 without changing their molecular strings or coordinates.

The tokenizer input manifest and FSQ serialization manifest identify this retained
collection. The binned export is traced to the same filtering project but contains
183,835,937 records. The difference is retained explicitly in the training comparison.

The final normalized-key rule was applied to training only. A separate validation
export used the earlier connectivity-block rule, retaining 1,779,945 of 1,802,906
records after 20,910 core-related and 2,051 PLINDER-only exclusions. Corpus test files
were not CASF/PLINDER-filtered. Similarity removal sets were constructed using hits
from both training and validation. The evaluated v2 Qwen runs have validation disabled
in their resolved configurations. CoordToken monitoring instead uses a fixed subset
of the original corpus validation collection, as recorded in its input manifest;
the benchmark overlap of this monitoring subset remains to be audited separately
from the cleaned validation export.

The benchmark filter recomputes identity after removing hydrogens and assigning
stereochemistry, whereas the corpus split parser uses the stored decoded molecule.
The resulting full keys differ on 1,812 training and nine validation records.
Benchmark filtering uses the recomputed keys. The supplied audit records zero decode
failures and zero missing keys in this filter.

\subsection*{Language-model training and generation}

The v2 models initialize from Qwen3-0.6B-Base or Qwen3-1.7B-Base.\cite{yang2025qwen3}
Molecular records pair a SMILES prompt with a coordinate-enriched SMILES sequence.
Binned records place three coordinate symbols and a delimiter after each atom,
using 256 interior bins over $-12$ to $12$ \AA{} and lower/upper overflow symbols.
FSQ records place one molecular code before the enriched SMILES and one code after
each atom. Geometry symbols are atomic tokens in the language-model vocabulary.
The geometry tokenizer remains fixed during language-model training.

Training minimizes next-token cross-entropy over SMILES and geometry tokens in complete
records packed into contexts of 4,096 tokens. Padding and the final prediction position of each record are
excluded from the loss, preventing a record from targeting the next packed record.
The effective batch is 144 packed sequences, using nine sequences per device,
two accumulation steps, and eight data-parallel workers. AdamW uses learning rate
$8\times10^{-4}$, $\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.95$, $\epsilon=10^{-8}$, and weight decay
0.1, with gradient clipping at norm 1.0. The rate warms up for 500 updates, stays constant, and decays linearly to zero
over the last 6,606 updates for binned coordinates or 4,106 updates for FSQ.
The data seed is 2025. Precision audits record bfloat16 model computation and FP32
master weights, optimizer states, and gradient reduction. All four evaluated
checkpoints complete one epoch; they are not subsequent GEOM fine-tuning checkpoints.
