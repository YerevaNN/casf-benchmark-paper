\section*{CoordToken and Qwen training records}

\subsection*{Geometry representation and tokenizer training}

CoordToken\cite{geikyan2026coordtoken} encodes canonical isomeric SMILES together
with centered heavy-atom coordinates. Coordinates follow the SMILES atom order;
non-atom tokens retain their graph information but carry no coordinate target.
The encoder and decoder each have eight transformer layers, width 1,024, 16 attention
heads, and feed-forward width 4,096. A 12-dimensional bottleneck quantizes each scalar
to two levels, giving 4,096 possible codes without a learned vector codebook.
One code is transmitted for each atom, together with one learned molecular register
code. The decoder receives these codes and SMILES identities and predicts Cartesian
coordinates. Training minimizes coordinate mean squared error over atom positions,
masking non-atom tokens and padding.

The benchmark uses the separate filtered release dated 20 October 2026, whose
checkpoint SHA-256 begins \texttt{37e5aa88c6e3de1d}. It is not the full-corpus model
used for the headline reconstruction results in the CoordToken paper. Both tokenizer
epochs use the same 187,398,101-record training manifest. Training begins from random
initialization and uses eight H100 GPUs, global batch 512, AdamW with peak learning
rate $5\times10^{-5}$, $\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.999$, dropout 0.05, and gradient
clipping at norm 0.1. Weight decay is 0.01 for matrix parameters and zero for biases
and normalization parameters. The first epoch warms up over 1,024,000 examples,
maintains the peak rate until 90\% of the epoch, and then decays to zero by a cosine
schedule. The second epoch resumes the model and optimizer state, restores the peak
rate, and decays over its final 20\%. Each epoch contains 366,012 updates. The initial
seed is 123 and the second-epoch shuffle seed is 124. Computation uses bfloat16 with
FP32 coordinate and quantization operations; the recorded environment is PyTorch
2.6.0 with CUDA 12.4. Monitoring uses a fixed 4,952-record development cohort.
Full checkpoint and data hashes are archived with the training records.

\subsection*{Training collection and benchmark exclusions}

The source collection contains 189,792,028 training records. After benchmark
exclusions, its largest components are PubChem3D (92,818,460 records), ZINC
(73,082,070), nablaDFT (8,623,394), GEOM (5,016,456), and ChEMBL3D (1,398,651).
Other records come from the OMol25 biological- and small-molecule collections,
SAIR, BindingNet, Binding MOAD, CrossDocked2020, HiQBind, DAVIS-3D, KIBA-3D,
Kinodata-3D, PLINDER, and SPINDR. Counts refer to conformer records, not distinct
molecular identities. The complete source counts before and after filtering are
provided in the companion \texttt{filter-per-source.csv}.

For exclusion matching, temporary molecular copies are uncharged using RDKit's
protonation-only mode and converted to canonical tautomers. Two keys are compared:
the connectivity block of the normalized InChIKey and a stereochemistry-independent
\texttt{HetAtomProtomer} hash. A match under either key is sufficient. The original
coordinates, charges, and stereochemistry of retained records are not rewritten.
For the 285 CASF core and original 23 PLINDER queries, matching molecules first form
an expanded query set. Molecules with ECFP4 Tanimoto similarity strictly greater
than 0.4 to this set are identified using 2,048-bit radius-2 Morgan fingerprints
without chirality, calculated on the original molecular forms. Their normalized
identity matches are also excluded. Previously identified exclusions from the
plain-identity and tautomer-based screens are retained. This procedure extends
beyond exact stereoisomer matching and does not apply a recursive similarity expansion.
For the 1,236 ref ligands, only the normalized identity exclusion is added.

The executed report assigns 2,201,663 removed records to the core-related category,
163,118 additional records to PLINDER, and 29,146 additional records to ref. These
categories are mutually exclusive in that order, leaving 187,398,101 records.
The tokenizer input manifest and FSQ serialization manifest identify this retained
collection. The binned export is traced to the same filtering project but contains
183,835,937 records. The difference is retained explicitly in the training comparison.

\subsection*{Language-model training and generation}

The v2 models initialize from Qwen3-0.6B-Base or Qwen3-1.7B-Base.\cite{yang2025qwen3}
Molecular records pair a SMILES prompt with a coordinate-enriched SMILES sequence.
Binned records place three coordinate symbols and a delimiter after each atom,
using 256 interior bins over $-12$ to $12$ \AA{} and lower/upper overflow symbols.
FSQ records place one molecular code before the enriched SMILES and one code after
each atom. Geometry symbols are atomic tokens in the language-model vocabulary.
The geometry tokenizer remains fixed during language-model training.

Training minimizes next-token cross-entropy over SMILES and geometry tokens in complete
records packed into contexts of 4,096 tokens. Padding and the final prediction position of each record are
excluded from the loss, preventing a record from targeting the next packed record.
The effective batch is 144 packed sequences, using nine sequences per device,
two accumulation steps, and eight data-parallel workers. AdamW uses learning rate
$8\times10^{-4}$, $\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.95$, $\epsilon=10^{-8}$, and weight decay
0.1, with gradient clipping at norm 1.0. The rate warms up for 500 updates, stays constant, and decays linearly to zero
over the last 6,606 updates for binned coordinates or 4,106 updates for FSQ.
The data seed is 2025. Precision audits record bfloat16 model computation and FP32
master weights, optimizer states, and gradient reduction. All four evaluated
checkpoints complete one epoch; they are not subsequent GEOM fine-tuning checkpoints.
