\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Conformer generators differ not only in whether they recover experimental bound
geometries, but also in how broadly and densely they distribute samples around those
geometries. Qwen provides broad geometric sampling and the strongest large-budget
CASF recovery among the evaluated generators. LoQI concentrates a larger fraction of
its PLINDER samples near the available experimental references. Geometric diversity,
reference coverage, sample precision, validity, and energy dispersion therefore
remain separate selection criteria rather than interchangeable measures of quality.

The pharmacophore experiments provide a direct bridge from this benchmark to library
construction. A mixture advantage observed for one protein-contact query definition
was smaller in the generator-blind extension, but the final four-family study shows a
consistent small-budget advantage for mixed Qwen/LoQI libraries. The equal allocation
has the highest numerical coverage in every query family at 20 attempts and remains
near the best family-specific coverage at 1,000 attempts, while retaining substantially
more geometric breadth than pure LoQI.

When one fixed production policy must be chosen without molecule-specific tuning, we
therefore recommend generating 50\% of the conformers with Qwen and 50\% with LoQI.
Within the objectives evaluated here, this is the best-supported balanced default:
it combines Qwen's breadth with LoQI's concentration, performs most strongly at the
smallest tested budget, and uses an equal split rather than fitting a continuous
mixture weight to the evaluation molecules.
The recommendation is not a claim of universal dominance. A 75:25 allocation is
statistically indistinguishable at the small budget and preserves more diversity,
while pure Qwen or 75:25 can be preferable at larger budgets. Downstream docking,
screening, and learning evaluations remain necessary before assigning the resulting
resource an application-specific advantage.
