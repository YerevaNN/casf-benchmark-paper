\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Molecular conformer generators provide three-dimensional structures for
shape comparison, pharmacophore screening, and structure-based molecular
modeling. For these applications, an ensemble is useful when it contains
conformations close to those a molecule adopts in the relevant experimental
setting. Previous work has examined how ensemble size, diversity, and energy
minimization affect recovery of bioactive conformations and downstream
screening performance.\cite{mcnutt2023conformers} These observations motivate
an application-specific evaluation of generated ensembles: how reliably does
a method recover an experimentally observed ligand geometry, and how much
sampling is required to do so?

Conformer generators approach this problem through different sampling
procedures. Distance-geometry methods combine molecular connectivity with
geometric constraints and experimental torsional preferences.\cite{wang2020etkdg}
Learned approaches include diffusion over torsional degrees of freedom,
continuous conformer fields, and models that combine molecular language
representations with coordinate generation.\cite{jing2022torsional,wang2024mcf,liu2025nextmol}
Low-energy conformer generation provides another objective, represented here
by LoQI and its associated computed ensembles.\cite{nikitin2025loqi}
These methods offer different ways to construct an ensemble, but their
relative performance must be established for the intended reference
structures and sampling regime.

Several properties of an ensemble need to be distinguished in that
comparison. Physical-validity checks assess molecular identity and local
geometric plausibility.\cite{buttenschoen2024posebusters} Geometric diversity
describes the spread of generated structures, whereas recovery measures
whether that spread includes a particular experimental conformation. An
ensemble can contain many distinct conformers and still miss the target.
Conversely, a large ensemble can recover the target through a small fraction
of its samples, even when its typical conformer is relatively distant.
These measurements do not, by themselves, establish binding affinity or
an equilibrium conformational population.

We therefore evaluate conformer generators from the perspective of
experimental-conformation recovery, interpreting their ranking jointly with
sampling budget, geometric diversity, and molecular complexity. The primary
comparison uses 94 CASF-associated ligand entries matched to ChEMBL3D, with a
larger matched collection used to examine the consistency and scope of the
findings. We compare classical and learned generators, including an
autoregressive Qwen 1.7B model, at a common candidate target and at smaller
sampling targets. A specified RMSD cutoff provides the principal comparison;
threshold curves show how that comparison changes with the required
structural agreement. Diversity is assessed across clustering radii, and
recovery is stratified by molecular size and flexibility.

A separate panel of 23 drug molecules extends the question from recovering
one experimental shape to covering multiple observed conformations. In this
setting, reference coverage and the fraction of generated samples near a
reference describe complementary properties of an ensemble. Together, the
two evaluations distinguish recovery by the best member of a sampled pool
from the concentration of that pool near observed structures. The intended
contribution is a comparative account of which generators serve these
objectives under the evaluated conditions, including where rankings change
with sampling, structural tolerance, and molecular complexity.
