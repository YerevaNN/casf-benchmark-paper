\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A flexible molecule can adopt different three-dimensional conformations
without changing its chemical identity. Molecular shape comparison,
pharmacophore screening, and docking therefore often use ensembles of
conformers rather than a single structure. For applications that depend
on a ligand's bound geometry, an important requirement is that the
ensemble contains a conformation close to an experimentally observed
structure. The probability of recovering such a structure depends on
both the generator and the number of conformers sampled. Previous work
has examined how ensemble size, diversity selection, and energy
minimization affect recovery of bioactive conformations and performance
in screening and docking.\cite{mcnutt2023conformers}

Recovery is distinct from physical validity and geometric diversity.
Validity checks assess molecular identity and local geometric
plausibility,\cite{buttenschoen2024posebusters} while diversity measures
variation within an ensemble. Neither property alone establishes that
a generated pool contains an observed ligand conformation. Recovery
can also depend on a small fraction of samples: an ensemble may contain
a close match even when most of its members are distant from the
experimental geometry. When several conformations have been observed,
the fraction of references recovered and the fraction of generated
samples near those references describe different aspects of ensemble
quality.

Classical and learned generators sample conformations through different
representations and procedures. Distance geometry combines molecular
connectivity with geometric constraints and experimental torsional
preferences.\cite{wang2020etkdg} Learned approaches include diffusion
over torsions, continuous conformer fields, and molecular language
representations.\cite{jing2022torsional,wang2024mcf,liu2025nextmol}
LoQI and its associated ChEMBL3D ensembles emphasize low-energy
structures.\cite{nikitin2025loqi} These approaches also differ in
refinement and filtering, which affect the ensembles available for
evaluation. In particular, minimizing the energy of an isolated molecule
does not directly establish its relevance in a protein
environment.\cite{mcnutt2023conformers}

Here, we evaluate classical and learned generators, including an
autoregressive Qwen model, by their recovery of experimentally observed
ligand conformations. The primary cohort comprises 94 CASF ligand entries
matched to ChEMBL3D, whose stored ensembles provide an additional
comparison.\cite{su2019casf,nikitin2025loqi} Recovery requires at least
one conformer within a specified root mean square deviation (RMSD) of
the experimental geometry after alignment. Candidate targets equal to
the stored ChEMBL3D count or 1,000 conformers per ligand assess performance
at different sampling allowances, while variation in the RMSD cutoff
assesses the required structural agreement. Geometric clustering and
comparisons by molecular size and flexibility characterize variation
in performance, with a larger reference cohort providing additional
observations.

A separate panel of 23 drug molecules with multiple experimental
conformations permits evaluation of reference coverage and sample
precision. Coverage measures how much of the observed conformational
variation is represented, whereas precision measures the fraction of
generated samples close to any available reference. These evaluations
compare generators in terms of their ability to recover observed
geometries and concentrate samples near them, under specified sampling
and matching conditions. The comparisons concern the evaluated generation
pipelines; they do not isolate the contribution of energetic refinement
or assume that experimental references exhaust the accessible
conformations of a molecule.
